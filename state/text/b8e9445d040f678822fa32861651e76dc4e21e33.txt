National Teen Driver Safety Week: HeyPolo Encourages Families to Make Safer Driving a Conversation, Not a Confrontation
Teen driving week is almost here. See what 2,320 crashes can teach families about building safer habits behind the wheel
Sinead
3 min read•October 14, 2026
With 2,320 people killed in crashes involving teen drivers in 2024, HeyPolo encourages parents and teenagers to review driving habits together and build safer behaviours behind the wheel.
October 2026 — Getting a driver's licence is one of the biggest milestones in a teenager's life. But while teenagers are excited about their newfound freedom, parents are often adjusting to a whole new set of worries.
As National Teen Driver Safety Week takes place October 18–24, family safety app HeyPolo is encouraging parents to take an active role in helping their teenagers become safer, more confident drivers.
According to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA), in 2024:
2,320 people were killed in crashes involving drivers aged 15–18.
752 teenage drivers died in traffic crashes.
33% of teen passenger-vehicle drivers involved in fatal crashes were speeding.
85,947 teenage drivers aged 15–18 were injured in traffic crashes.
Helping teenagers develop safer driving habits
Passing a driving test is just the beginning. Developing good driving habits takes time, experience and support.
HeyPolo's helps families review driving behaviours including speeding, phone use, harsh braking, rapid acceleration and cornering.
Rather than using driving scores to criticise or punish young drivers, HeyPolo encourages parents to review the information with their teenagers, talk about what went well and identify areas for improvement.
Families can set small, achievable goals together, such as reducing phone distractions or improving their driving score over the following month.
More independence for teenagers, more reassurance for parents
HeyPolo also offers crash detection, location sharing and arrival alerts, helping families stay connected as teenagers gain independence.
Designed around privacy and trust, the app encourages families to agree on how and when they use safety features.
The goal isn't to watch every journey. It's to give teenagers the freedom to grow into confident drivers, while giving parents some reassurance.
Five ways parents can support safer teen driving
Keep practising together. Continue practising in different driving conditions after your teenager passes their test.
Talk about distractions. around phone use and passengers.
Review driving habits regularly. Use driving safety information to have constructive conversations about speeding, braking and distractions.
Set goals together. Focus on progress and developing better habits rather than punishment.
Lead by example. Demonstrate the safe driving behaviours you want your teenager to develop.
Parents can also access the National Safety Council's free DriveitHOME resources at nsc.org/NTDSW, including guidance on speeding and distracted driving.
About HeyPolo
HeyPolo is a privacy-first family safety app built by the team behind Surfshark. With features including location sharing, arrival alerts, Driving Safety Scores and crash detection, HeyPolo helps families stay connected while respecting their independence.
