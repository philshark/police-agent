Can You Get A Permit at 14? Yes, In These Six U.S. States
Course enrollment isn't the same as permit eligibility. Six states let 14-year-olds apply, but the application is only the beginning. Behind every permit age sits a holding period, practice hours, and an adult in the passenger seat for months.

Key takeaways
Alaska, Arkansas, Iowa, Kansas, North Dakota, and South Dakota set 14 as the minimum permit age.
A permit at 14 authorizes supervised driving only, with a fully licensed adult in the car.
Johns Hopkins researchers reported in 2006 that graduated licensing cut fatal crashes involving 16-year-old drivers by 11% on average.
Driver education can start before a state will issue a permit, as Texas and Florida both show.
Alaska asks for 40 supervised hours, and 10 of those have to happen at night or in bad weather.
Farm and hardship permits follow separate rules and do not change a state's standard learner age.

A driving school in Little Rock takes enrollment at 14. Pull up the Arkansas DMV site on the next tab and the number matches, but only because Arkansas is one of six states that actually allow it. Alaska, Iowa, Kansas, North Dakota, and South Dakota round out the list.
Everywhere else, a fourteen-year-old is still a year or more away from a permit.
The six states where 14-year-olds can apply and what the permit age actually means
Six states set 14 as the minimum permit age for standard learner entry. What differs after that threshold is everything else: what the permit is called, what a teenager has to pass before it is issued, and how long they hold it before the next stage.

Iowa is the cleanest example of how a state fills in those blanks. A 14-year-old there applies for an instruction permit after passing written and vision tests, so the birthday alone does not produce the permit. The Insurance Institute for Highway Safety notes that some age-14 pathways attach driver education conditions on top of the age, which is worth knowing before your teenager counts the days to a birthday that may not be the only requirement.
When can you get your learner's permit and what it lets a teenager do
A learner's permit authorizes only. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration describes as a learner's permit, an intermediate license, and a full license, and says a learner's permit allows driving only while supervised by a fully licensed driver, as of November 2021. A 14-year-old with a permit in Iowa and a 16-year-old with a permit in another state hold the same kind of document, with the same core restriction.
Alaska shows what the learner stage asks of a family in practice. The Insurance Institute for Highway Safety lists a six-month holding period there, with 40 hours of supervised driving, including 10 hours at night or in inclement weather. Those 40 hours land on you, most likely in a parking lot on a Sunday and on the same four residential streets until your teenager stops braking too hard at the stop sign. Ten of them have to happen in the dark or in weather you would rather avoid, which is the part most families underestimate when they plan around a birthday.
The restrictions carry a documented safety record, and the numbers argue for paying more attention to them than to the starting age. Johns Hopkins researchers reported in 2006 that graduated driver licensing programs reduced by an average of 11%. The supervision requirement is doing work.

At what age can you get your permit if driver education starts earlier
Course enrollment and permit issuance are two separate dates, and driving schools advertise the earlier one. Texas makes the split obvious. A teenager there can begin driver education before turning 15, while the Texas Department of Public Safety issues the teen learner license to applicants aged 15 to 17 who have completed the required classroom instruction. Asking at what age can you get your permit in Texas returns 15, even though the coursework can already be underway.
Florida works the same way on a slightly different calendar, with preparation starting at 14 and a half and permit issuance beginning at 15.
When can you get a permit through a farm or hardship exception
Some states issue restricted permits to teenagers below the standard learner age, and those pathways do not change the state's general permit age. Oklahoma.gov states that a farm-restricted pathway took effect on November 1, 2023, for qualifying applicants aged 14 to 17 who live or work for compensation on a farm.
Oklahoma's standard learner stage begins later than 14, so the farm exception does not add a seventh state to the list above. A restricted permit comes with its own conditions on where and why a teenager may drive, and your state licensing agency is the place to read them rather than a summary anywhere else.
At what age can you get a permit in your state, and what to check before applying
Your state licensing agency page is the only source that settles the question for your family, because the answer to at what age can you get a permit changes by state and changes over time. Before your teenager applies, you might find it worth checking the current page for the minimum age, the required tests, whether driver education has to be underway or completed, the documents and fees, the supervision rules, the practice hours, and the holding period before the next stage. National summaries, including this one, go stale when a legislature amends a statute.

FAQ
At what age can you get your permit?
The age depends on the U.S. state. Six states allow standard learner entry at 14: Alaska, Arkansas, Iowa, Kansas, North Dakota, and South Dakota. Many other states begin at 15 or later.
What age can I get my permit?
Applicants should check the current licensing agency for the state where they will apply. Minimum age, driver education requirements, and testing rules differ by state and can change.
When can you get a permit?
Eligibility begins when an applicant meets the state's minimum age and its application conditions. Those conditions can include a written test, a vision test, or enrollment in driver education.
When can you get your learner's permit?
Alaska, Arkansas, Iowa, Kansas, North Dakota, and South Dakota set 14 as the minimum age. Other states commonly begin later, and some allow driver education coursework to start before the permit age.
At what age can you get a permit?
The range runs from 14 in the six states named above to 15 in Texas and 15 and a half in California. [CHECK BEFORE PUBLISHING]
Can you drive alone with a permit at 14?
No. NHTSA defines the learner stage as supervised driving with a fully licensed driver. Each state sets who qualifies as a supervisor and what additional restrictions apply.
Does starting driver education mean you have a permit?
No. Course eligibility and permit issuance can happen at different ages. Texas allows coursework before 15 and issues the teen learner license from 15, and Florida allows preparation from 14 and a half with permits issued at 15.
Does Oklahoma let you get a permit at 14?
Oklahoma has a farm-restricted pathway for qualifying applicants aged 14 to 17 who live or work for compensation on a farm, effective November 1, 2023. Oklahoma's standard learner permit begins later.
