Knowing when your child is ready to stay home alone
There is no age that settles it. Most US states name no minimum at all, pediatric guidance points at 11 or 12, and neither one tells you whether your child would open the door to a stranger or freeze at a smoke alarm.
Phil
10 min read•September 1, 2026
My keys were already on the counter, and the errand was fifteen minutes each way. My kid looked up from the sofa and said she'd rather stay put. Which is the exact moment a lot of parents start searching what age can a child stay home alone, hoping for a number that settles it.
There isn't one. The Wisconsin State Law Library puts it plainly, saying there is no agreed-upon age when a child can stay home safely, and most places leave the call to parents. So the decision rests on three separate things: what your local law permits, how your child behaves when small problems come up, and what you're actually asking of them. Twenty minutes at 4pm is a different ask than a whole evening. Age gives you a starting point and nothing more.

What age can a child stay home alone according to child health guidance
Child health organizations tend to land on 11 or 12 for a short stretch alone during the day. HealthyChildren.org, the parenting site run by the American Academy of Pediatrics, says child experts typically agree that 11 or 12 years old is an appropriate age to stay home alone for a few hours. Safe Sitter, which trains children in home-alone skills, puts the usual window at 10 to 12 for brief periods.
A recommendation from a pediatric body is not the same thing as a legal minimum. Where your state, county, or country names an age in statute, that number governs, whatever any organization advises. And no single age can answer the follow-up questions, which are the ones that matter more: how long, what time of day, whether a younger sibling is in the house, and who your child reaches if the power cuts out.
So when you ask what age can my child stay home alone, hold the 11-to-12 range as a rough floor for a first short trial rather than a green light.
What age can a child stay home alone by state, and what those rules actually are
Most U.S. states name no minimum age at all. A handful do, with Illinois setting the highest threshold in the country at 14 and others sitting anywhere from six upward. FindLaw's overview lists these thresholds, and its summary conflicts with other sources on several entries, including Colorado and North Carolina, so every line needs checking against the state's own current material before you rely on it.
Even where no number exists in law, child protection agencies can still treat inadequate supervision as neglect. Maryland's statute makes it a crime to leave a child younger than eight unattended. Arizona designates no statutory age and instead investigates neglect case by case. Two different legal shapes, and a parent in either state can end up answering the same questions.
When you look up the legal age to stay home alone where you live, check what kind of rule you've found. A statute sets a hard minimum. Agency guidance describes what caseworkers look for. A parental-judgment standard leaves the decision with you and holds you accountable for the outcome.
Why 11 or 12 keeps coming up when parents ask what age can a kid stay home alone
The number isn't arbitrary. A review of brain development through early adolescence, hosted by the National Institutes of Health, describes a shift right around this point. Around the age of 12 years, the review says, adolescents decrease their reliance on concrete thinking and begin to show the capacity for abstract thinking, visualization of potential outcomes, and a logical understanding of cause and effect.
Picturing an outcome you haven't seen yet is what an unfamiliar emergency demands. A child who can only reason from what's in front of them will handle a burnt slice of toast and freeze at smoke coming from behind the oven. A child who can run the next three steps forward in their head unplugs it and calls you.
That review describes a pattern across many young people, not a certificate for yours. Development runs on its own timetable, and a 13-year-old can be less prepared than a 10-year-old in the same street. Which is why the rest of this comes down to what you've watched your own child do.
Signs my child is ready to stay home alone that you can actually watch for
Maturity is hard to assess as a single quality, so it helps to break it into behavior you've already seen. Maturity is hard to assess as a single quality, so it helps to break it into behavior you've already seen, the same way you would with any decision about how much independence to give.
A review of decision-making capacity in young people, published on the NIH's PMC archive, sets out four capacities involved in making a decision: communicating a choice, understanding, reasoning, and appreciation. Sit with your child for ten minutes and you can hear all four, or hear that one is missing.
Ask what they'd do if the doorbell rang and they weren't expecting anyone. A child who says "I wouldn't open it, I'd text you" has communicated a choice. A child who can explain why the delivery driver leaving the parcel is fine and the stranger asking to come in isn't has done the reasoning. A child who shrugs and says "I'd probably just open it" has told you something useful too.
Positive signs you might look for before a first trial:
They've said they want to try, unprompted.
They can call you and one nearby trusted adult, from memory or a written card.
They follow agreed household rules when you're in another room and can't see them.
They can describe what they'd do if the power went out or the smoke alarm sounded.
They can make a snack without the oven, the stovetop, or anything you've ruled out.
They can name what they'd do when the afternoon starts dragging.
Signs it might be worth waiting a while:
They go quiet or change the subject when you raise it.
They've told you the house feels strange when it's empty.
They agree to every rule instantly, with no questions, which usually means they haven't pictured it yet.
They insist nothing could possibly go wrong, which is a different thing from confidence.
They can't say who they'd contact if your phone rang out.
That last one on the first list deserves more room than it usually gets. A clinical review of children left in self-care, indexed on PubMed, found conflicting results across the literature, with outcomes running from learning to be independent and responsible through to loneliness, boredom, fear, accidental injury, and impairment of the parent-child relationship. The same arrangement helps one child and unsettles another. So how your child feels about the hours is evidence, not a soft add-on to the practical checks.
There's a line worth watching between a child who's comfortable and one who's certain they're invincible. Comfort sounds like "I'll be fine, I'll text you when you leave." Arrogance sounds like "why would I even need to call you." The second one tends to skip the plan.

What age can a child stay home alone overnight, and why duration changes everything
Twenty minutes tells you very little about an evening. A child alone at 4pm on a Tuesday has daylight, neighbors who are up, and a parent who can be back before anything develops.
Twenty minutes tells you very little about an evening. A child alone at 4pm on a Tuesday has daylight, neighbors who are up, and a parent who can be back before anything develops. The same child at 11pm has none of that, and adding a younger sibling changes the job again, because now they're responsible for someone else's decisions as well as their own. Frequency matters too. An occasional errand and a standing arrangement where your child lets themselves in every weekday ask different things of them.
Apply the same five questions to each scenario and the differences show up quickly: how long you're gone, what time of day it is, how fast your child reaches help, whether anyone depends on them, and how they feel about it beforehand.
Overnight sits in its own category. Northern Ireland Direct, the official government information service there, advises that children under 16 should not be left alone overnight. Nothing about a successful short daytime trial carries over to a night on their own, and the legal age to stay home alone overnight is worth checking separately for your own jurisdiction, since some places address overnight care and short absences differently.
What age can a child stay home alone UK and Australia guidance says
Neither country sets a single national number, and the guidance you find depends on which government body publishes it.
For the UK, Northern Ireland Direct states there is no legal age limit for leaving a child on their own, but it's an offense to leave a child alone if this puts them at risk. The same guidance says children under 12 are rarely mature enough to cope in an emergency and shouldn't be left at home alone for a long period. Readers in England, Scotland, or Wales may want to check their own nation's guidance rather than assume the Northern Ireland wording applies.
In Australia, the New South Wales Department of Communities and Justice says the law provides no clear direction as to what age a child can be left at home alone, and asks parents to use their own judgment based on family circumstances alongside the age and maturity of their children. That guidance covers New South Wales only, so anyone asking what age can a child stay home alone Australia will want their own state or territory government. Canada works the same way, with Manitoba and New Brunswick setting province-specific rules rather than a country-wide one.
What to teach my child before they stay home alone
Once the law is clear and the readiness signs are there, the rehearsal matters more than the conversation. You might find that practicing the awkward parts out loud does more than explaining them, and that most of what you rehearse belongs in the emergency plan your family shares rather than in a one-off talk before an errand.
Things families often run through first:
Have your child call you, then call a nearby trusted adult, while you're both still in the house.
Agree on when they call emergency services rather than calling you.
Act out the doorbell ringing and the power cutting out, so the first time isn't the real time.
Move medicines and anything hazardous somewhere they don't reach by accident.
Settle which foods and appliances are in and which are out.
Write down the time you'll be back, and be back then.
The American Red Cross runs a Babysitting Basics Online course designed primarily for youth ages 11 and older, covering household safety and emergency response, which some families use to build the practical skills before a first absence. Then start small. Fifteen minutes nearby, and a real conversation afterward about how the house felt.

A checklist before my child can stay home alone that includes the check-in plan
Agree on the check-in before you leave rather than working it out by text once you're gone. NBC Boston reported in 2024 that regular check-ins are worth having for absences longer than 15 to 30 minutes. One message at a set time beats four in an hour, because four teaches your child that you're anxious and that the plan doesn't hold.
If your child wants to share their location during the trial, let them pick the terms. Their choice of how precise it is, and when it starts and stops, is part of what makes them agree to it a second time.

Their choice of how precise it is, and when it starts and stops, is part of what makes them agree to it a second time. Sharing an approximate area or pausing it altogether is a legitimate answer from a 12-year-old, and the settings kids control themselves are the ones that survive past the first week. Pressing past that answer costs you more than the map gives you.
A check-in supports the plan and replaces none of it. A message at 4:30 does nothing about a smoke alarm, which is what the rehearsals and the neighbor two doors down are for.
When should I let my child stay home alone
You can check what your jurisdiction actually requires, watch how your child handles the doorbell rehearsal, pick a fifteen-minute errand close by, and stay reachable while you're gone. Four things you control, none of which depend on hitting a birthday. If the short trials go well and the arrangement becomes a regular one, the next thing to settle is an after-school routine that holds without you in the room.
Waiting is a real answer too. A child who goes quiet when you raise it, or who can't yet say who they'd call, is telling you where you are. Six months is not a long delay, and nothing is lost by asking again in the spring.
When the trial does happen, an agreed check-in time and a shared plan carry most of the weight. FAQ
Is it legal for a child to stay home alone?
Legality depends on jurisdiction. Some U.S. states name a minimum age in statute, while most apply neglect or child endangerment standards instead and assess each situation individually. In the UK and Australia, no national minimum age exists, though leaving a child at risk can be an offense. Parents can check the current rule published by their own state, province, or national government before a first absence.
At what age can I let my child stay home alone?
HealthyChildren.org, published by the American Academy of Pediatrics, says child experts typically agree that 11 or 12 years old is an appropriate age to stay home alone for a few hours. Local law may set a different minimum. Duration, time of day, whether siblings are present, and the child's own comfort with the arrangement all change the answer beyond age alone.
How do I know if my child is ready to stay home alone?
Observable behavior tells parents more than age does. Signs include wanting to try, following household rules unsupervised, contacting a parent and a nearby trusted adult without prompting, describing what they would do if the doorbell rang or the power failed, and managing boredom or fear during a short absence. Reluctance is a reason to wait rather than a hurdle to talk through.
What is the legal age to stay home alone?
No universal age applies anywhere in the world. A minority of U.S. states set a numerical minimum, with Illinois the highest at 14, while others rely on neglect standards. Readers in the U.S. can verify their state's current rule against official state material. Readers elsewhere can check the guidance published by their own national or regional government.
What is the legal age to stay home alone overnight?
Overnight rules vary by jurisdiction, and some places address overnight care separately from short absences. Northern Ireland Direct advises that children under 16 should not be left alone overnight. Readiness for a brief daytime period does not establish overnight capability, since night hours change how quickly a child reaches help and how comfortable they feel in the house.
What age can a child stay home alone UK?
Northern Ireland Direct states there is no legal age limit for leaving a child on their own, but it is an offense to leave a child alone if this puts them at risk. The same guidance says children under 12 are rarely mature enough to cope in an emergency, and children under 16 should not be left alone overnight. Families in England, Scotland, and Wales can check their own nation's guidance.
What age can a child stay home alone Australia?
The New South Wales Department of Communities and Justice says the law provides no clear direction as to what age a child can be left at home alone, and asks parents to use their own judgment based on family circumstances alongside the age and maturity of their children. That guidance applies to New South Wales. Families in other states and territories can consult their own government.
Can an older child look after a younger sibling?
Sibling care asks more of a child than being alone does, because they become responsible for another person's decisions during an emergency. Some jurisdictions set a separate age rule for supervising younger children, distinct from any home-alone threshold. Parents can verify the local rule for sibling supervision rather than assuming the home-alone age covers it.
