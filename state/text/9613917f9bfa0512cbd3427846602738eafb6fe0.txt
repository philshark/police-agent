SOS alerts FAQ
What does an SOS alert do in HeyPolo?
Tapping once sends an emergency alert with your live position to everyone in your chosen group. All members are notified together, and your position keeps updating every five seconds while you're sharing. Whoever sent that alert can see which contacts received it, so nothing travels anywhere unseen.
How do I send an emergency alert on HeyPolo?
Tap SOS, then slide the control across to send. The screen shows who the alert will reach before you slide it. Your group receives the notification with your live position within seconds. Practising once with your family helps, especially with a grandparent or a young teen, because a button you've pressed before feels different from one you've only read about.
What if I tap SOS by accident?
A tap on its own sends nothing. It opens the emergency screen, and the alert only goes out when you deliberately slide the SOS control across, so a press in a pocket or a bag never reaches anyone. Close the screen and that's the end of it. If a real alert does go out and you're fine, you can tell your group straight away, and everyone who was notified sees that too.
What can my group actually do when they get an alert?
Whatever fits the moment. They can call you, message the group to work out who's nearest, drive over using your live location, ask a neighbour or a friend closer by to check on you, or call emergency services and pass on exactly where you are. Because everyone is notified together, nobody assumes someone else has it covered.
Does a panic button app replace calling emergency services?
No. Please call 911 or your local emergency number when someone needs medical, police, or fire response. HeyPolo reaches your family, not dispatchers. Both matter, and they work well together, since relatives who know your position can meet responders, unlock a door, or drive over.
Can I use HeyPolo as a safety alarm app for an elderly parent?
Yes, and older parents tend to accept it because control stays theirs. A single tap notifies every adult child at once, position included. Private mode lets your mother or father share nothing on ordinary afternoons while keeping that emergency button ready. Low battery alerts help too, flagging a phone that's about to go quiet.
Is HeyPolo a family locator app?
HeyPolo is a location-sharing app, which reads differently in practice. Nobody watches anybody. Each person decides who sees them, at what precision, and for how long, and both sides of any sharing arrangement can see that it's happening. Knowing somebody got home replaces asking, without turning care into oversight.
What is the best emergency alert app for a whole family?
Judge candidates on three things: whether the person sharing keeps control, whether every member can raise an alert, and how pricing behaves as relatives join. HeyPolo gives sharing control to whoever is being shared with, opens one tap SOS to everybody invited, and holds one price however many people you add.
How much battery does it use?
Running in the background with sharing on, HeyPolo typically uses about as much battery as a messaging app left open, around 4% a day. Position refreshes every 5 seconds while you're watching the map and every 30 seconds when the app is closed, which is the deliberate part: constant polling is what drains most location apps.
