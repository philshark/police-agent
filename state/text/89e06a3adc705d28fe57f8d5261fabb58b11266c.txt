Car Insurance Went Up With No Tickets? Check Your Apps
Lawsuits say popular apps, including the family location app Life360, quietly collected driving data that ended up with insurers. Here's how it works.

Why location sharing apps are the perfect data source
A location sharing app has permission to track your phone all day, every day. That's what makes it useful for families. It's also what makes it valuable to data companies.
With the right software built in, an app like Life360 can tell when you're driving, how fast you go, how hard you brake and whether you pick up your phone. That turns into a driving score, and driving scores can be sold to insurers. Per Reuters, the federal case alleges Allstate tracked exactly these things through apps including Life360.
The catch: a phone can't always tell whether you were driving or just a passenger.
What the lawsuits say

Apps named include the family location app Life360, plus GasBuddy, Fuel Rewards and Allstate's own Routely.

Allstate disputes the claims and says people share driving data through these apps only after a clear notice and an explicit opt-in. Both cases are ongoing.
Check your apps in 2 minutes
Open your location sharing app's privacy settings. Look for "driving data," "driving insights," "sharing with partners" or "Do Not Sell or Share," and switch it off.
Request your driving report. Consumer reporting companies like LexisNexis and Verisk must give you a free copy of the file they keep on you. It shows whether driving data is on your record.
Choose an app that doesn't sell your data. The simplest fix is a location app whose business doesn't depend on what it collects.
