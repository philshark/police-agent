The Worry Every Parent of a Teenager Knows
It's not the big stuff that gets you. It's the silence.
Sinead
3 min read•June 10, 2026
Nobody prepares you for the particular kind of anxiety that comes with parenting a teenager.
Not the dramatic stuff - the late nights, the arguments about curfews, the heart-in-mouth moments you imagined when they were small. The thing nobody warns you about is the silence. The phone that goes to voicemail.
97% of parents according to research from the University of Michigan - say they actively try to help their teens become more independent. They let them make more choices, give them more responsibility, push them to handle things themselves. And yet, a quarter of those same parents admit they are the single biggest barrier to their child's independence. Not because they don't want to let go. Because letting go is terrifying.
The three texts every parent sends
If you have a teenager, you probably recognise these:
Did you get there?
Are you on your way home?
Let me know when you're back.
They're not unreasonable requests. They're just the bare minimum of "I need to know you're alive." But ask any teenager how they feel about receiving them, and you'll get a look that could strip paint.
About half of parents in the US now use location-tracking apps to monitor their teenagers' movements. But researchers have raised concerns that constant surveillance can actually damage the trust between parent and child, creating a false sense of security while quietly eroding the relationship it was meant to protect. There has to be a better way.
If Life360's always-on tracking is part of what's bothering you, it's worth seeing how the best Life360 alternatives compare on exactly this trade-off.
The thing teenagers actually need
Here's the uncomfortable truth: teenagers need to feel trusted in order to become trustworthy. The research on this is consistent. Adolescence is the period when young people develop their sense of identity, their autonomy, and their capacity to make good decisions. That development requires real-world experience — including some degree of risk and independence.
The two things you actually need to know
When your teenager is out, the questions running through your head usually come down to two things:
Did they get where they were going?
Are they okay?
That's it. Not a live feed of their location every thirty seconds. Not a full breakdown of their route. Just those two things.
The app that feels different

Every person who uses HeyPolo chooses when they share their location, how precisely, and with whom. There's no constant background tracking. No always-on monitoring. One parent who uses the app put it simply: "It's changed how we talk about going out. It's not a negotiation anymore. We both know the app is there if we need it.”
It's not just teenagers either, plenty of adult children feel the same pull about living far from parents.
The constant worry doesn't fully disappear - that's just parenting. But it does get quieter. And sometimes, that's everything.
