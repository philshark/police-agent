Frequently asked questions
Is HeyPolo an elderly tracker app?
People searching for an elderly tracker app usually want something an older parent will keep. HeyPolo is a location-sharing app, so your mom installs it, joins a group, and sets her own level of detail. Nothing reaches you without her agreement, and she can pause any time.
What is the best app to track elderly parents?
Whichever one your parent keeps on their phone. Hidden tools and forgotten pendants get abandoned quickly. HeyPolo runs on a phone that's already in their pocket, shows both sides who is sharing, and lets them pick a general area rather than a precise point when precision feels like too much.
Is HeyPolo a tracking app for dementia patients?
No. HeyPolo isn't a medical device, and it has no wandering alerts or clinical monitoring designed for dementia care. Some families use it in early stages alongside professional support. Anyone who might not remember agreeing to share is better served by purpose-built care equipment.
Does it work without a smartphone?
No. Everyone in a group needs an iPhone or an Android phone with the app installed. There's no pendant, no separate GPS unit, and no monthly device fee on top of the subscription.
Does HeyPolo drain the battery?
Sharing uses location services, so expect a draw comparable with a maps app sitting in the background. Low battery alerts warn the group when a phone falls below its threshold, which is frequently what explains an unanswered afternoon.
Does HeyPolo detect falls?
Fall detection isn't part of what we do. Your parent gets one-tap SOS instead, so a single press sends an emergency alert with live location to everyone in their group. No number to find, no address to recite.
How is this different from a medical alert device?
A pendant connects your parent to a call center. HeyPolo connects them to you and your siblings directly, on hardware they already carry. Plenty of families run both, one for clinical emergencies and one for ordinary days.
Can my parent stop sharing whenever they want?
Yes. Private mode pauses everything while keeping them in the family group, and the group sees that pause. Sharing can also be set to a window, like a drive to an appointment, after which it switches off without anyone remembering to do it.
How many people can join one family group?
As many as you like. Unlimited groups, unlimited invited members, all under a single subscription. Your brother, your aunt, and the neighbor who checks in on Tuesdays each join at no extra cost.
