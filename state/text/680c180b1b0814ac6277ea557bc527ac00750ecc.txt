What is real-time location sharing?
Real-time location sharing is a continuous, consent-based stream of a person's position between people who have agreed to see each other. Instead of pinging someone for a one-off pin, both phones publish position updates on a schedule and HeyPolo draws them on a shared map.
Under the hood your phone blends GPS, Wi-Fi and cell signals into a single fix, then sends it only when you've actually moved enough to matter. That's why the map keeps up on a bike ride but sits quietly while you're at your desk.
Every stream is reciprocal and revocable. Sharing starts with an invitation, shows a persistent indicator while it's on, and stops the second either side turns it off, no lingering history in someone else's app.

Consent, in plain terms
Nobody can see your live position unless you accepted their request first, and acceptance is per person, not per app.
While sharing is on, a persistent indicator sits at the top of your map so it can never run quietly in the background.
Turning it off is a single tap and takes effect immediately; the other side stops receiving updates and keeps nothing new.

Frequently asked questions
How often does the map update?
Every 3–10 seconds while you're moving, and less often when you're stationary to save battery.
Can someone track me without my knowledge?
No. Sharing requires an explicit accept from you, and a persistent indicator shows whenever it's active.
Does it work if my phone is offline?
Your last known position stays on the map with a timestamp, and updates resume the moment you're back online.
How much battery does it use?
Typically under 4% per day thanks to adaptive update intervals and motion detection.
How long is my location kept?
Live points are transient; history is kept for 30 days on Premium and can be deleted at any time.
Can I share for a limited time only?
Yes, choose 1 hour, 8 hours, or until you stop it manually.
Do both people need Premium?
No. Real-time sharing works between any two HeyPolo accounts.
