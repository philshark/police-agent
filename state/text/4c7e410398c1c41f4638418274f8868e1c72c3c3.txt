Why families are leaving Life360
The price went up. In its , Life360 confirmed it raised US prices for new subscribers. Silver went from $7.99 to $9.99 a month, and Gold from $14.99 to $16.99. 85% of paying US families are on Gold, so $16.99 is the realistic number to compare against.
The free plan still exists, but it caps you at 2 place alerts and 2 days of location history.
The free plan is paid for with ads and data. According to the same letter, around 80% of Life360's US users can be shown ads, and ad revenue hit $22.0 million in the quarter, up 315% on the year. Its "other revenue" also grew 25% to $11.6 million, mostly from selling what its filings call aggregated, non-personally identifiable data. A new Subscription Tracker feature, which you have to opt into, gives the company a view of your finances for personalized offers.
In other words, if you're not paying for Life360, your attention and your data are funding it.
The privacy track record. In 2021, that Life360 sold precise location data to data brokers, which Life360 later said it would stop doing. In 2024, a flaw in its login API . Then in 2025, the Texas Attorney General's lawsuit against Allstate and its subsidiary Arity named Life360 as one of the apps allegedly feeding location data into insurance decisions.
That's the kind of thing parents don't forgive. In our own survey, if it sold their data.
It does too much. For a family that just wants to know everyone got home, crime reports, credit monitoring and constant upsells get in the way. Some users also notice , especially on older Android phones.
How we evaluated these apps like Live360
Several people on our team used each app for a week as paying customers. We set up a group, invited each other and created another group just for our family members and used it for real things like school pickups and late drives home.
We scored each one on how easy it was to set up and get people into a group, and how useful it was day to day once the novelty wore off.
Then we read every privacy policy, tried to delete our own data, and checked what each company says it does with what it collects.

What happens to your data

2. Apple Find My
App Store rating: 2.8 out of 5 (about 18,000 ratings)
If everyone in your family has an iPhone, Find My is already installed and free. You get live location and arrive or leave alerts, and Apple deletes your location after 24 hours.
What we liked
Setup took no time, because there's nothing to download or sign up for.
Locations are encrypted, and Apple says it doesn't sell them.
What we didn't
It's Apple-only. Life360 says 46% of its paying families mix iPhone and Android, and none of them can share through Find My.
We didn't find the alerts easy to tune, and reviewers also complain about locations drifting while someone stands still.
There's no location history, driving feature or SOS in the app.

3. Glympse
App Store rating: 4.8 out of 5 (about 49,000 ratings)
Glympse shares your location through a link that expires after a set time, or when you arrive. The person you send it to opens it in a browser, so they don't need the app.
What we liked
It was the quickest way we found to share one trip with someone outside the family app.
When the timer runs out, the share ends.
What we didn't
Shares max out at four hours, and longtime users say a recent update stopped sharing whenever Glympse isn't open on screen.
It has no place alerts, no history and no always-on family sharing.
In the Mott Poll, 25% of parents said they check their adult child's location during a rideshare or taxi trip.
That's the test we didn't get to run: a college student sending one Glympse link from the back of an Uber at 1 a.m., and nothing else all week. We think plenty of 19-year-olds would take that deal.

4. OtoZen
App Store rating: 4.6 out of 5 (491 ratings)
OtoZen is a driving safety app first. It has automatic crash detection with a 24/7 emergency response team for eligible US users, speed alerts tied to the road's limit and texting-while-driving warnings.
What we liked
Speed alerts show where your teen sped and by how much, which gives you something specific to talk about.
The student driver log exports a PDF for the DMV. We didn't find that in any other app here.
What we didn't
Most of the value lives in the car. If your teen isn't driving yet, you're paying for features you won't use.
Family members' location history and drive reports are kept for 3 months. We'd rather that were shorter.
A week wasn't long enough to see that log do its real job. Learners in most states must record supervised hours before their test, and plenty of families still do it on paper.
Our bet is that over a full permit period, it's the feature parents of a 15-year-old would use most.

5. GeoZilla
App Store rating: 4.5 out of 5 (about 51,000 ratings)
GeoZilla is the closest match to Life360's feature set, with place alerts, location history, an SOS button and driver coaching.
It also pairs with GPS trackers for kids who don't have a phone yet.
What we liked
The alert settings go deeper than most apps here, so you can choose which places and which people trigger what.
What we didn't
On a highway drive, location updates came several seconds apart. At that speed it left gaps of several hundred yards between points on the map, so we rarely knew exactly where the car was.
Reviewers describe the same lag, short trips logged as hundreds of miles, and refunds that were hard to get after the 3-day trial.
It asks for names, birth dates and photos of every family member, children included, though it says it doesn't sell personal data.

It runs on a watch, so a child doesn't need a phone to be on the family map.

8. FamiSafe
App Store rating: 4.1 out of 5 (about 4,400 ratings)
FamiSafe is a parental control app with location built in. It blocks apps, filters content, sets screen-time limits and gives you driving reports for teens.
What we liked
The content blocking is thorough. Reviewers who need a phone properly locked down say it does that job.
What we didn't

We found that the support was email-only support and had trouble uninstalling it.
Its policy says it may collect clipboard data, and it shares data with Wondershare companies in China, Canada, Japan and Hong Kong.

9. Bark
App Store rating: 4.3 out of 5 (about 36,000 ratings)
On its Premium plan, Bark scans your child's texts, email and social apps for signs of bullying, predators or self-harm, and alerts you when something looks wrong. Both plans include location check-ins.
What we liked

Which Life360 alternative should you choose?
Start with the reason you're leaving, and pick the app that fixes that one thing.
All iPhones, and you don't want to pay: Apple Find My.
A teen who just got their license: HeyPolo for everyday sharing with crash detection, or OtoZen if you want an emergency team and a DMV log.
A child under 12 with a watch: Findmykids.
Screen time or online safety is the real problem: FamiSafe or Bark, alongside a location app.
Everyone else: HeyPolo.
That last group is most families, couples and friend groups. If you want live location, place alerts and crash detection for less than Life360 Gold, with no ads and no data sold, HeyPolo is the one to try. You've got 30 days to change your mind.
Life360 alternatives: FAQ
What is the best alternative to Life360?
For most families, HeyPolo. It covers live location, place alerts, SOS and crash detection for $4.99 a month billed annually, and one subscription covers everyone you invite. It doesn't show ads or sell your data.
Is there a free app like Life360?
Yes. Apple Find My is free for families who all use iPhones. Glympse is free for sharing your location for a set time. GeoZilla, iSharing and Life360 itself have free plans, but they hold back alerts and history.
Which Life360 alternatives work on both iPhone and Android?
HeyPolo, OtoZen, GeoZilla, Findmykids, iSharing, FamiSafe, Bark and Glympse all work across both. Apple Find My only works between Apple devices, which rules it out for mixed families.
Does any Life360 alternative have crash detection?
Yes. HeyPolo and OtoZen both detect crashes automatically and alert your group. OtoZen adds a 24/7 emergency response team for eligible US users.
Does Life360 sell your data?
Life360 says it no longer sells precise location data. Its 2026 filings show it still earns revenue from selling aggregated, non-personally identifiable data, and around 80% of its US users can be shown ads.
How do I switch my family from Life360 to another app?
