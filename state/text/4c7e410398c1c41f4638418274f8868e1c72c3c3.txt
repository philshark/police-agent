What are location-sharing apps?
Location-sharing apps let you share your with other people through your phone or other devices. Most people use these apps to stay connected with family and friends, coordinate plans, or make sure that loved ones are safe and sound.
These apps usually use signals like GPS (Global Positioning System), Wi-Fi, and mobile networks to pinpoint where you are and show it on a map. Here’s how they work:
You turn on location sharing in the app.
You choose who you want to share your location with.
Your device updates your location in the background.
The app sends that data to the servers.
The app shows your location on a map.
Beyond just showing real-time location, many modern location-sharing apps now include additional features like places and arrival alerts, emergency notifications, precision control, and even driving insights.
Why people are leaving Life360
While Life360 works well for some users, others feel it falls short in certain areas. Let’s run through the main reasons people start exploring alternatives to Life360.
Privacy and data sharing concerns
Life360 collects location data to run features like live location, place alerts, location history, and driving insights. This is pretty standard for location-sharing apps. But what raises concerns is how that data is reportedly used beyond those core features, and by companies other than Life360. The company has faced criticism and lawsuits over its privacy and data handling practices.
Here are a few examples:
Alleged selling of precise location data
According to , Life360 reportedly shared precise location data of millions of users with data brokers. Those brokers then sold it on to third parties, including hedge funds, advertising companies, and government agencies.

Life360 has since said it would move away from selling precise geolocation data toward “aggregated” data analytics instead.
Allstate Privacy Lawsuit
In a 2025 Texas Attorney General lawsuit against insurance company Allstate and its subsidiary Arity, Life360 is named as one of the apps that allegedly used Arity’s tracking technology to collect location data.
Allstate then used this data for underwriting decisions like renewing policies, raising rates, or denying coverage.
Data Breach Incidents
In 2024, a hacker by exploiting a flaw in its login API.
Just months later, Life360 detected unauthorized access to a Tile customer support platform in a separate breach.
Both incidents exposed names, addresses, emails, and phone numbers.
Excess tracking and feature overload
If you’re just looking for a simple way to stay connected or get peace of mind, Life360 can be a little too overwhelming. Many reddit users explain that the continious updates and detailed visibility make it feel more controlling, or surveillance like, which can sometimes create unnecessary pressure and make everyday movement and routines feel overly monitored.
Plus, for families with young children or elderly relatives, some of its more advanced features like driving analytics may not get much use. For them, these extra features simply make the app more complex rather than more useful.
Higher cost
Life360 does offer a free plan, but it comes with some serious limitations. The ones you’ll likely feel most are the caps on place alerts (only up to two locations) and location history (limited to just two days).
Additionally, the more advanced, and appealing, features like driving insights, crime reports, and emergency dispatch require a paid subscription. 3 - Find My. The Dedicated Apple and iOS Option

Location alerts: get notified when your partner leaves for work, your child arrives at school, or your parent gets home safely;
Privacy protection: your and your loved ones’ location data is encrypted on Apple’s servers and only kept for 24 hours;
Dual purpose: works for both people and devices — one app to help you keep up with family members as well as your iPhone, iPad, AirTag, and more.
Cons:
Apple-only: Find My is designed for Apple devices, which can be limiting if your family or friend groups use a mix of Android and iPhone;
Fewer safety features: the app is fairly lightweight and doesn’t include extras like driving insights, crash detection summaries, and emergency alerts;
Limited controls: you don’t get more advanced controls like approximate location sharing or temporary sharing boundaries, which you’ll often see in dedicated apps like Life360 and HeyPolo.
Find My is included for free on Apple devices. You don’t need to pay to access its location-sharing features.

FAQ
Is GeoZilla better than Life360?
No, GeoZilla isn’t exactly better than Life360. Both apps offer similar core location-sharing functionalities, with a strong focus on safety and driving-related features. But each has its own strengths and drawbacks.
Life360 is generally more feature-rich, though it has raised concerns about data sharing. GeoZilla, on the other hand, offers excellent cross-platform support and works with GPS trackers like wearables, but comes with a higher price tag.
Why is Life360 falling?
Life360 isn’t necessarily falling, but more people are starting to explore alternatives for a few reasons.
On the privacy front, Life360 has received some backlash over how it handles user data. At the same time, the app may feel too excessive and complex for those who just want simple location sharing. And since a lot of its features sit behind paywalls, the cost can add up pretty quickly.
Does Life360 have any competitors?
Yes, Life360 has a number of competitors. Apps like HeyPolo, iSharing, Find My, GeoZilla, and FamiSafe are all solid alternatives to Life360, each offering different takes on location sharing.
What app is safer than Life360?
It depends on what you mean by “safer.” Generally, HeyPolo is considered a more privacy-oriented alternative to Life360. Built by the Surfshark cybersecurity team, it doesn’t sell your location data, use it for advertising, or share it without your consent.
