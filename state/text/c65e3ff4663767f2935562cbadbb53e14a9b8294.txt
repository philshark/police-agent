What is location history?
Location history is a record of where someone has been over recent days, drawn only from trips they chose to share. In HeyPolo, that view covers the past 7 days and then clears on its own.
You can look back at a route, check when somebody reached school or work, and confirm a drive ended where it was headed. Nobody turns up in there by surprise. Each person decides who sees their movements, whether the view shows an exact spot or a wider area, and everybody can tell what they're sharing at any moment.
Parents of teenagers use it to skip the “where are you?” text. Adult children of a parent living alone reach for a quieter version of the Sunday night welfare call. Anyone waiting on a late commute gets an answer without sending one more message into a silent group chat.

Location history FAQ
Is there an app to track location history?
Yes. HeyPolo shows the past week of trips for anybody who chose to share them, so you can see routes, arrival times, and finished journeys without asking. Sharing rests on agreement, which means each person decides who gets a view and how detailed it is. Older records clear themselves.
Is a location history tracker the same as a location sharing app?
They overlap, though the controls sit in different hands. Apps that track location history vary widely on who holds those controls, and some hand them entirely to the account owner. HeyPolo gives them to whoever is sharing, so your teen or your dad picks their own level of detail, per group, and both sides can see the arrangement.
Do I need a GPS tracker with location history?
Probably not, if everybody already carries a phone. A GPS tracker with location history adds one more gadget to charge and keep charged, and says nothing at all about agreement. HeyPolo runs on the handset in their pocket, refreshes every 5 seconds while sharing is on, and shows the week's routes to the people invited.
How far back does location history go in HeyPolo?
Seven days. Older trips drop away on their own, so nothing needs deleting by hand and no long archive of anyone's movements piles up. Whatever somebody shared inside that window appears as a route with times attached, and a stretch of private mode simply shows as a pause.
Can someone see my location history without me knowing?
No. Sharing starts with your agreement, and you can check what each group has access to at any point. Switch on private mode and your sharing stops right away, with a note in the group that you've paused. Nothing runs quietly behind your back.
What is the best location history app for a family?
The one everybody agreed to keep on their phone. That's the standard HeyPolo aims for, with general-area sharing for anyone who'd rather not be pinned to an exact address, private mode for anyone wanting a break, settings that differ per group, and a single subscription covering however many people you invite.
How do I stop sharing my location history?
Private mode pauses your sharing with one tap. You may also set a time window before you begin, so it ends by itself when the school run or the night out finishes. Either way, your group sees the change, and nothing carries on in the background once you've stopped.
How often does my position update, and how accurate is it?
Every 5 seconds while the app is open and you're watching the map, and every 30 seconds when it's closed or minimised. That slower background interval is deliberate, since checking every few seconds around the clock is what drives most of the battery drain in location-sharing apps. Accuracy averages around 5 metres, tighter outdoors with a clear view of the sky and looser indoors, underground, or between tall buildings.
Does it work the same on iPhone and Android?
Yes, with no difference in features, performance, or what you can access. Everyone in a group sees the same thing regardless of which phone they carry, which matters in most households since they're rarely all on one platform. Background sharing typically costs about as much battery as a messaging app left open, and less again if you share a general area or use time windows.
