Want To Remove Life360? Follow These Steps
Deleting your account is just the last step. To actually clear your data from Life360, you'll need to back up what you need, cut off data collection, unlink your devices, cancel your subscription, and file a deletion request first.
Phil
5 min read•August 16, 2026
Life360 is a popular location-sharing app used by families and friends to stay connected.
However, growing concerns about how location data may be collected, shared, and monetized have led some users to .
For people who consider precise location history especially sensitive, simply trusting that an app is no longer being used may not provide enough reassurance
Deleting your Life360 Account
You can delete your Life360 account, but account deletion should be only one part of the process. If you want as much of your personal information as possible removed from Life360’s systems, you'll first migrate anything you need, stop further data collection, disconnect linked services and devices, cancel your paid subscription, and submit a formal data-deletion request.
You should also ask Life360 to identify any information it must retain for legal purposes, explain why it is being retained, and delete it when the applicable retention period ends.
The following step-by-step guide explains how to migrate away from Life360 while reducing the amount of personal data that remains on its servers.
TL;DR, Delete Your Account Like this:
Migrate service → export data → stop collection → opt out → disconnect partners and devices → cancel subscription → submit formal deletion request → delete account → revoke permissions → uninstall → verify and follow up.
Important To Know Beforehand:
A paid Life360 account does not create a blanket legal requirement for Life360 to retain all your data for a fixed period. Some limited records, such as transaction, tax, fraud-prevention, dispute, or legal-claim records, may need to remain, but Life360 does not publish a specific retention period for every category. Its current policy says retention varies according to purpose, legal obligations, disputes, audits, legal defenses, and legitimate interests.
Life360’s law-enforcement guidelines say it generally retains:
Raw location data: approximately 30 days.
“Dwell” location data: up to 18 months.
These are Life360’s operational retention periods, not legal minimums, and the guidelines do not say they apply only to paid accounts.
GDPR applies primarily based on where you are located and where the processing occurs, not simply because you mention GDPR. If GDPR does not apply, use the same request under your applicable U.S. state privacy law. Life360 currently recognizes access, portability, correction, deletion, opt-out, and appeal rights under numerous U.S. state laws.
Step-by-step Life360 migration and deletion checklist
1. Identify everything you need to migrate
Before deleting anything, make a list of the information and functionality you want to migrate:
Family and Circle members
Saved Places and addresses
Emergency contacts
Crash-detection settings
Roadside-assistance information
Driving reports or safety records
Location history you legitimately need
Tile, Jiobit, or Pet GPS devices
Connected services such as Uber, identity-protection services, employer benefits, or bank accounts connected through Subscription Tracker
Receipts and subscription invoices
Do not change your Life360 email address or phone number yet. Life360 may use them to verify your access and deletion requests.
2. Migrate your family to the replacement service
Set up the replacement service before shutting down Life360:
Create the new family or household group.
Add every family member.
Migrate Places, alerts, emergency contacts, and device settings.
Test location sharing and emergency features.
Confirm everyone can access the new service.
If minors use Life360, ensure their parent or guardian handles the migration and deletion process.
Do not assume that deleting the Circle owner’s account deletes the other members’ accounts. Life360 says each Circle member must delete their own account.
3. Download and migrate your personal data before deletion
In the Life360 app, go to:
Settings → Privacy → Additional Data Rights → Request Your Personal Information
Life360 warns that its ordinary in-app disclosure may not include all technical information. Therefore, also email and request a complete access and portability package covering:
Raw location data
Dwell or summarized location data
Driving events and driving reports
Movement, speed, acceleration, and motion data
Device identifiers and advertising identifiers
IP addresses and application activity
Account and Circle information
Saved Places
Messages, photographs, and user-generated content
Emergency-assistance information
Inferences and profiles created about you
Subscription, cancellation, and payment-related records
Customer-support records
The sources from which your data was obtained
The recipients or categories of recipients that received it
The retention period for each category
The legal basis for each category of processing
Under GDPR, where applicable, you may request data you provided in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format and ask Life360 to transfer it to another provider where technically feasible.
Wait until the export is complete and readable before deleting your account.
4. Immediately stop additional collection
While waiting for your export:
Open your phone’s privacy settings.
Change Life360 location permission to Never or Don’t Allow.
Disable:
Precise location
Physical activity or motion access
Bluetooth access, if you no longer need a connected device
Background app refresh
Contacts access
Photos access
Notifications
Log out of Life360 on other phones or tablets.
Turn off Drive Detection and location sharing inside Life360.
Life360 states that disconnecting location and movement access in device settings, logging out, uninstalling the app, and deactivating applicable products will stop further location collection. However, these actions do not delete historical data or your account.
5. Opt out before deleting the account
Before account deletion, use:
Settings → Privacy → Your Privacy Choices
Select every available option to:
Opt out of personalized advertising.
Opt out of sale or sharing of personal information.
Limit the use and disclosure of precise geolocation.
Opt out of business-partner disclosures.
Opt out of Traffic & Retail Insights.
Disconnect data feeds under Trusted Partners.
Life360’s current policy says that, where permitted and depending on settings, it may disclose precise geolocation, movement, driving, device, and related data to certain partners for advertising, analytics, monetization, and other commercial uses.
Do this even if you intend to delete the account immediately. It creates a record that you withdrew permission and objected to further sharing.
6. Disconnect third-party accounts
Delete or disconnect every external integration, including where applicable:
Uber
Bank or credit accounts connected to Subscription Tracker
Plaid connections
Identity-theft protection
Roadside-assistance providers
Emergency-dispatch services
Employer-benefit programs
Social-media logins
Apple or Google account access
Tile, Jiobit, or Pet GPS devices
Also review the third party’s own privacy settings. Life360’s deletion process cannot necessarily delete copies independently retained by third-party controllers under their own policies. Life360 specifically states that third-party services are governed by their own privacy policies.
7. Transfer, reset, or deactivate physical devices
If you have Tile, Jiobit, Life360 Pet GPS, or similar devices:
Transfer the device to its new owner or account before deleting the original account.
If it will not be transferred, factory reset or deactivate it.
Remove the battery where possible.
Delete the device from Life360.
Confirm that it no longer appears online.
Life360 warns that active Tile and Pet GPS devices may continue to generate anonymous location observations from nearby community devices even after account deletion. It recommends transferring, deactivating, or disabling the device before deleting the account.
8. Delete unnecessary information manually
Before submitting the final account deletion, manually delete or remove anything the app permits you to remove:
Saved Places
Profile photograph
Optional demographic information
Emergency or medical information
Unneeded Circle invitations
Messages or photographs
Connected devices
Trusted Partners
Linked bank accounts
Subscription Tracker records
Old support attachments
Optional contacts and addresses
This does not replace the formal deletion request, but it reduces what remains accessible in the live account while Life360 processes the request.
9. Cancel the paid subscription at the billing source
Cancel the paid membership before deleting the account.
Depending on where you purchased it:
Life360: Settings → Account → Cancel Membership
Life360 website: Manage Membership → cancellation section
Apple: Cancel under Apple subscriptions
Google Play: Cancel under Google Play subscriptions
Employer or reseller: Contact the employer or reseller
Save:
Cancellation confirmation
Final invoice
Last billing date
Subscription expiration date
Screenshots showing auto-renewal is disabled
Deleting the app, leaving a Circle, or deleting the account does not by itself cancel the paid membership. Life360 also says that subscription cancellation normally takes effect at the next renewal date and does not automatically delete the account.
10. Submit a formal deletion and restriction request
Send the request through the in-app Additional Data Rights section and by email to . Using both methods creates a better record.
Use wording such as:
Subject: Access, portability, objection, restriction, and deletion request
I am requesting the deletion of my Life360 account and all personal data associated with it. This request applies to my account identifiers, location history, raw location data, dwell data, driving events, movement data, device information, advertising identifiers, inferred information, saved Places, Circle information, communications, support records, marketing profiles, and information held by processors or service providers.
I withdraw any consent previously provided and object to further processing for advertising, marketing, profiling, analytics, product development, research, commercial insights, sale, sharing, or partner monetization.
Please delete all personal data that is no longer necessary for the original purpose for which it was collected.
If you believe that any information must be retained, please:
Identify each retained category and specific data element.
State the exact legal basis and, where applicable, the statute or legal obligation requiring retention.
State the purpose for retaining it.
State the retention period and scheduled deletion date.
Restrict the data from all other processing, including marketing, analytics, profiling, product development, and commercial disclosure.
Store the retained data separately or otherwise limit access to personnel who require it for that legal purpose.
Confirm that the retained data will not be restored to active systems from backups.
Please delete or irreversibly anonymize personal data in active systems, archives, logs, and backups according to your deletion schedule. Any anonymization must prevent me from being identified or re-identified.
Please notify applicable processors and recipients of my deletion, withdrawal, objection, and restriction requests, and provide me with the recipients or categories of recipients notified.
Please also confirm:
The date my account was deleted.
The categories of data deleted.
The categories retained.
The legal basis and expiration date for every retained category.
The expected date on which backup copies will be deleted or rendered inaccessible.
Whether any data was disclosed to independent advertising, analytics, research, insurance, retail, or monetization partners.
My account information is:
Full name:
Account email:
Account phone number:
Country and state of residence:
Relevant service: Life360
This request is made under the GDPR where applicable and under any applicable U.S. state privacy law.
Under GDPR, deletion can be refused only to the extent processing remains necessary for reasons such as a specific legal obligation or the establishment, exercise, or defense of legal claims. GDPR also requires data minimization and storage limitation—not indefinite retention merely because data might someday be useful.
11. Delete the Life360 account
After your data has been migrated and your subscription has been canceled:
Open Life360.
Go to Settings.
Select Account.
Select Delete Account.
Confirm that you understand the deletion.
Continue to the email-verification step.
Open the verification email.
Follow the deletion link.
Complete the CAPTCHA.
Save the deletion confirmation.
Life360 says account deletion permanently deletes history and driving events and cannot be undone.
Repeat this process for every Circle member. Deleting one account does not delete everyone else’s accounts.
12. Revoke remaining access and delete the app
After confirming account deletion:
Delete the Life360 app.
Revoke Life360 access in Apple, Google, Facebook, or any other login provider.
Remove Life360 from your phone’s list of location-enabled applications.
Reset your mobile advertising identifier if desired.
Delete Life360 cookies and website data from your browsers.
Enable Global Privacy Control in browsers you use.
Remove saved Life360 passwords from your password manager only after the deletion is verified.
Check Apple or Google subscriptions again to verify that renewal remains canceled.
13. Verify the deletion
Keep a small evidence folder containing only:
Your original request
Export confirmation
Subscription cancellation
Account deletion confirmation
Life360’s response
Any list of retained data and deletion dates
After a reasonable period, try a password reset using the former account email or phone number. Do not recreate the account; use this only as an indication of whether an active account still exists.
If Life360 sends marketing after deletion, unsubscribe and reply that consent was withdrawn and the account was deleted.
14. [IMPORTANT] Follow up on retained data
If Life360 says it retained information, ask:
Is this retained because of an actual legal obligation, a current legal hold, or merely a business preference?
What exact statute or contractual requirement applies?
Why is each retained field necessary?
Can the record be reduced to a transaction number, date, amount, and tax information rather than retaining location, device, or behavioral data?
Is the retained data blocked from marketing, analytics, profiling, and product development?
What is its automatic deletion date?
When will backups expire?
Who can access the retained records?
A vague answer such as “for legal or business purposes” should be challenged. Ask for a category-by-category answer.
15. Escalate if necessary
Where GDPR applies, Life360 should generally respond without undue delay and within one month, although a valid extension may be available for complex requests. If it refuses, it should explain why and inform you of your complaint rights.
If the response is incomplete:
Reply and identify each unanswered item.
State that you are appealing the decision.
If GDPR applies, complain to your local data-protection authority.
If a U.S. state privacy law applies, use Life360’s appeal process and then contact your state attorney general if necessary. Life360 specifically directs denied U.S. requests to for appeal.
Use A Better, Privacy-First, Location-Sharing app
Moving away from Life360 involves more than uninstalling the app or pressing Delete Account.
If any records must remain for legitimate legal purposes, ask that they be minimized, restricted from unrelated use, and automatically deleted once the applicable retention period expires.
At HeyPolo, we believe location sharing should not require families to sacrifice control over their personal information. Privacy and security should be built into a service from the beginning, not treated as settings users must hunt down after their data has already been collected.
Whatever alternative you choose, review its privacy practices carefully and select a service that collects only what it needs, clearly explains how information is used, and makes it easy to migrate or delete your data when you decide to leave.
FAQ
Does deleting the Life360 app delete my account?
No. Deleting or uninstalling the app from your phone does not necessarily delete your Life360 account or the information already associated with it. To permanently close the account, use Life360’s account-deletion process and complete any required verification steps.
Does deleting my Life360 account cancel my paid subscription?
Not necessarily. Your subscription may be managed by Life360, Apple, Google Play, an employer, or another provider. Cancel the subscription through the original billing source and save the cancellation confirmation before you delete your account.
Should I export my Life360 data before deleting my account?
Yes, if you want to retain any account information, location history, driving reports, saved Places, subscription records, or other available data. Request and review the export before deleting your account, because deletion may be irreversible and could remove your access to that information.
Can Life360 retain some data after I delete my account?
Potentially. A company may retain limited records when necessary to comply with tax, accounting, fraud-prevention, dispute-resolution, security, or other legal obligations. However, that does not necessarily justify keeping every category of account, location, or behavioral data.
Ask Life360 to identify exactly what it is retaining, the reason and legal basis for retaining it, how access will be restricted, and when it is scheduled to be deleted.
Should I turn off location access before deleting my account?
Yes. Turn off location, precise location, motion, physical-activity, Bluetooth, contacts, and other permissions that Life360 no longer needs. You should also disable location sharing and Drive Detection within the app where possible.
These actions help stop additional collection, but they do not delete previously collected information. You must still submit an account and data-deletion request.
What happens to data shared with Life360’s partners?
Deleting your Life360 account may not automatically delete information already received and independently retained by another company. Before closing your account, disconnect linked services and ask Life360 which recipients received your personal information.
Where applicable, ask Life360 to notify its processors and other relevant recipients about your deletion request. You may also need to contact independent third parties directly.
Does deleting one Life360 account delete the entire Circle?
No. Each person’s account and personal data may need to be handled separately. Every Circle member who wants to leave should migrate the information they need, cancel any individual subscription, and submit their own deletion request. A parent or guardian may need to manage requests involving a child’s account.
