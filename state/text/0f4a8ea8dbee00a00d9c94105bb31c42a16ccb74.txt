Best Location-Sharing Apps For iOS and Android in 2026
Whether you’re looking to stay connected with family, know your elderly parents are safe, or coordinate plans with friends, a reliable location-sharing app can make all the difference.
Choosing the right one starts with understanding how apps vary in pricing, features, and privacy controls. This guide breaks down some of the best location-sharing apps out there, including HeyPolo.

How we tested these location sharing apps
We looked at each of these apps and asked 6 different questions:
Does the app support live location updates at least every 30 seconds?
Anything slower than 30 seconds and you're seeing where someone was, not where they are at the cost of battery life.
What level of control does the user have in sharing their location?
Turn sharing on and off on your terms, share an approximate area instead of an exact pin, and control front and center, not buried in settings.
Does the app use location data for any other purposes?
No selling or sharing location history with advertisers or data brokers.
Can the app be used cross-platform?
Features, alerts, and the interface is a consistent experience between iPhone and Android, not just technically available on both.
How easy is the app to set up and use daily?
Quick setup, everyday actions and emergency functions in a few quick taps.
Does the app provide value for money?
Does the price tag justify the answers to the question above?
What we found when trying to answer these questions is what helped us establishe the best app for each use case.
Best location-sharing apps - our top picks
Here’s what we think works best for a select handful of use cases you might find yourself in.
HeyPolo: Best for privacy-first location sharing
Most location sharing apps assume continuous tracking is the default situation. They’ll assume your privacy is something you opt into if you dig through settings. HeyPolo, built by the team behind the VPN company Surfshark, works the other way around: sharing is off until you turn it on.
Against the rest of this list, all of which either run in the background by default or have location built into a broader social or parental-control product, HeyPolo is the one built specifically around consent rather than always-on visibility.
HeyPolo users create Groups and add their contacts in there. One person subscribes and invites the rest for free, and each member sets their own visibility inside every Group they're in: exact location, general area, or private.
Locations refresh every 5 seconds while real-time location sharing is on, and the real differentiator is that any sharing window you set closes itself automatically.
Best for: privacy-conscious families, couples, and friend groups who want real-time reassurance without anyone feeling watched around the clock, whether that is a night out or the twenty minutes when kids walk to school.
Key strengths:
Arrival and departure alerts
Exact, approximate, or private modes
Trip history log
Low battery alerts
Driving speed monitoring
One subscription, unlimited members
HeyPolo is app-based only. There's no physical GPS tracker or wearable option, so it won't cover pets, cars, bags, or anyone without a smartphone the way a hardware tracker would.
There's also no desktop app. Everything happens on the phone, so checking on someone from a computer isn't currently an option.
Plans start at $1.99/mo billed annually ($5.99/mo billed monthly), with a 30-day money-back guarantee.
Choose HeyPolo when: everyone you're sharing with already carries a smartphone and you'd rather set the terms of sharing yourself than have it tracked by default.
Check HeyPolo's current pricing and plans at heypolo.com/pricing.
Life360: Best For Features
Life360 is the most feature-dense app on this list.
It’s built less upon simple location sharing and adds on driving safety and family monitoring and wearables. It features crash detection, driving behavior reports, and physical Tile tracker integration on top of the basic map and alerts every other app in this roundup offers.
Family members join a Circle, capped at six people, and Life360 tracks location plus the driving data most apps don't touch: speed, phone use behind the wheel, and hard braking, all on the paid tiers.
Since acquiring Tile, Life360 also covers physical objects.
You can drop a tracker in a bag, on a keychain, or on a pet collar and see it in the same app as your family's locations.
The feature worth calling out on its own is crash detection. If Life360 registers a collision above 40 km/h, it can alert you and your Circle automatically, without anyone needing to send a message first.
Best for: families who want driving and safety data layered on top of location, not just a pin on a map.
Key strengths:
No-show alerts
Physical Tile tracking
Driving behavior reports
Crash detection over 40 km/h
Free plan available
Place and arrival alerts
Reports have linked Life360 to selling or sharing user location data with third parties, including data brokers and insurance companies. Sharing also defaults to always-on, and Circle members have limited control over when their location is visible, which is part of why so many people are migrating away from Live360.
Pricing is per member rather than per household, and Circles are capped at six people, so costs climb fast for larger families or friend groups. The free plan is thin too: place alerts are limited to two locations, and most of what Life360 is known for (driving reports, crash detection, Tile tracking) comes on the paid tier. Some users also report noticeably higher battery drain from the constant background tracking.
Plans start at $5.99/mo, with a free tier and a 7-day trial on paid plans.
Choose Life360 when: you want driving safety data and physical trackers bundled in with location sharing, and you're comfortable trading some privacy control for that depth.
Review Life360's current plans and pricing at life360.com/plans-pricing, or read HeyPolo's side-by-side breakdown of how the two compare on privacy.
Google Maps: Best for Free, No Frills Location Sharing
Google Maps is the free, already-installed option in this roundup, built for a quick share rather than ongoing family monitoring. Where HeyPolo and Life360 ask you to set up Groups and manage privacy modes, Google Maps just needs a Google account you're probably already signed into.
Sharing works on a timer you set yourself, anywhere from 15 minutes to indefinite, and the person you share with can turn on notifications for updates.
It also doubles as a coordination tool: send your destination and Google Maps shows your ETA alongside real-time traffic and transit conditions, so whoever's waiting on you can watch the trip instead of just a static pin.
Best for: one-off shares, like letting someone know you're walking home from the station or checking in during a solo trip.
Key strengths:
Adjustable share duration
Live ETA and traffic
Works on iPhone and Android
No setup or download needed
Simple for non-tech-savvy relatives
Completely free
Google Maps isn't built for ongoing family safety. There's no emergency sharing, no arrival alerts for when someone gets home, and no parental controls, so it won't replace a dedicated safety app if that's what you're after.
Group management is thin too. There's no family dashboard, no group-based permissions, and no way to set different visibility levels for different people, so it works best for sharing with one person at a time.
Choose Google Maps when: you want a quick, free way to share your location for a specific trip or moment, not an always-on family safety system.
Find My: Best for iOS
Find My is the only app in this roundup that doesn't need to be downloaded at all, since it comes on every iPhone and Mac. It's built for people who want location sharing and lost-device tracking in one place, without adding a third-party app to devices that already have it covered.
Find My works because it's part of iOS itself rather than a separate service running on top of it, so sharing runs in the background without the battery cost you'd expect from a dedicated app. The same app also tracks AirTags and other Apple devices, therefore you can track your AirTag in the same app that you track the kids.
Best for: Apple households, sharing location with family and friends who are also on iPhone or Mac.
Key strengths:
Pre-installed, no download
Low battery impact
AirTag and device tracking
Family Sharing group support
Simple for all ages
Completely free
Find My sticks to the basics. There's no emergency alerts, no parental controls, and no crash or collision detection, so it won't cover the safety features apps like Life360 are built around.
It's also built for Apple devices first. Location sharing works best when everyone in the group is on iPhone or Mac, and it offers little for families or friend groups with a mix of Android and iOS, or for anyone who wants approximate-location or temporary-sharing controls.
Choose Find My when: everyone you're sharing with is already on an iPhone or Mac and you don't want to add another app for something Apple already does, though it's worth checking which app is better for your situation first.
iSharing: Best Cross-Platform Location Sharing App
iSharing is in the middle of this roundup: more features than Find My or Google Maps, but lighter than HeyPolo or Life360. It's built for families and friend groups split across iPhone and Android who want more than a basic map pin without taking on a full-featured, higher-priced app.
Location sharing with iSharing works the way most apps in this list handle it, group members see each other on a shared map, with the option to send an SOS alert that includes audio for faster, clearer communication in an emergency. On certain plans, iSharing also adds a driving monitor that flags speeding, harsh braking, and phone use behind the wheel.
Best for: families or friend groups who need solid cross-platform sharing without paying for HeyPolo or Life360's full feature set.
Key strengths:
SOS alert with audio
Driving behavior monitoring
Full iOS and Android support
Speeding and harsh-braking flags
Phone-use-while-driving detection
Free plan available
The free plan is ad-supported in a way that affects core features. Viewing your family's location history or driving reports means watching an ad first, which isn’t, at best, very inconvenient.
Most of what makes iSharing useful, place alerts, emergency alerts, location history, is gated behind higher-tier plans, so the free version covers basic sharing and not much else. Refunds are also tighter than most: you only have 24 hours after purchase to request one.
Plans start at $3.99/mo across three paid tiers, on top of the free option.
Choose iSharing when: your group is split between iPhone and Android and you want driving and SOS features, and you’re ok with ads.
GeoZilla: Best For Monitoring Elderly Parents
GeoZilla is the only app in this roundup that doesn't require the person being tracked to own a smartphone. Every other entry here works by putting the app on both phones. GeoZilla still does that, but it also sells standalone GPS trackers built for exactly the situation where an elderly parent doesn't carry, or doesn't reliably use, a phone.
The app itself works on opt-in consent: you enter a phone number, GeoZilla sends an SMS request, and once the recipient approves, their location shows up on your map in real time, with arrival and departure alerts and up to 336 hours of location history.
The tracker is what makes this entry worth its own spot on the list.
GeoZilla sells physical GPS devices, including a category built specifically for elders, that pair with the app over a cellular connection rather than Wi-Fi or Bluetooth.
Clip one to a keychain or bag and you get the same real-time location and geofencing alerts as the app, without needing your parent to charge a phone, keep an app running, or remember to carry it in the first place.
For an older parent who's lost a phone before, or never used one comfortably, that's the difference between GeoZilla actually working and it sitting unused.
Best for: adult children monitoring an elderly parent who doesn't reliably carry or use a smartphone, a situation where app-based location sharing for elderly parents only works if the phone stays in a pocket.
Key strengths:
Standalone GPS tracker devices
Elder-specific tracker category
Crash detection alerts
Geofencing and arrival alerts
336-hour location history
Free plan available
Pricing is layered in a way the other apps here aren't. The app itself is somewhere between roughly $4.44/mo on a long-term commitment and around $19.99/mo month to month, and that's before the tracker: devices are $60 to $250 upfront, plus their own cellular plan on top of the app subscription. Budgeting for GeoZilla means accounting for hardware and service costs together, not just an app subscription so keep that in mind, too.
Choose GeoZilla when: the person you're trying to keep tabs on won't reliably carry a smartphone, and you need a physical tracker as the fallback.
Snapchat: Best For Fun & Staying Social
Snapchat isn’t the app you’d expect to see on this list.
It isn't a location-sharing app first, it's a social app that happens to include one of the most-used maps for seeing where your friends are and what they're doing. Compared to the rest of this list, Snap Map is the only option that puts location into an existing social feed instead of a dedicated safety or family tool.
Snap Map shows your friends as Bitmoji avatars on a live map, choosing between sharing only while the app is open or continuously in the background.
It's also woven into the rest of Snapchat: the same map surfaces friends' public Snaps and Stories tagged by location, so you're seeing what people are actually doing nearby. For a teenager, that visibility is part of the same after-hours social load that shows up at home as school pressure.
Sharing is off by default and limited to mutual friends you specifically choose. If you don't open Snapchat for 24 hours, you stop showing up on the map until you open it again, and Ghost Mode hides your location from everyone at any time.
Best for: friend groups and teens who already live on Snapchat and want to see where friends are alongside the rest of their social feed. It stops short of proper group location sharing, since there is no way to build a defined circle with its own rules.
Key strengths:
Bitmoji live map
Location-tagged Snaps and Stories
Ghost Mode privacy toggle
Friends-only by default
24-hour auto-expiring visibility
Completely free
Snap Map has none of the safety features built into dedicated apps on this list. There's no arrival or departure alerts, no SOS button, no driving reports, and no way to set up a family Circle, so it's not a substitute for an app built around monitoring or safety.
It's also locked to an active Snapchat account. Getting someone's location means both people need to be on Snapchat, be mutual friends, and actively use the app, which doesn't work well for parents or older relatives who aren't already on the platform.
Choose Snapchat when: you and your friends are already using the app daily and want casual, social location sharing rather than a dedicated safety tool.
Findmykids: Best For Young Kids
Findmykids is built around a different assumption than the rest of this list: the person being tracked might be too young to have their own opinion about it, or even own a phone. Most of these apps assume two people consent to share with each other, but Findmykids is a one-way parent-to-child tool, and it's the only app here designed to work with kids who don't carry a smartphone at all.
The setup splits into two parts. Parents use the Findmykids app, while the child's side has either a companion app (Pingo) on their own phone or, more often for younger kids, a dedicated GPS watch that pairs with the parent's app over cellular.
The watch handles calls, an SOS button, and location on its own, so a 6-year-old doesn't need a phone for a parent to know where they are.
The feature worth flagging on its own is "sound around," which lets a parent briefly listen to the audio around the child's device. It's a real safety tool for checking in without a phone call, but it's also the app's most debated feature, since it works without notifying the child at the moment the sound is shared.
Best for: parents of young kids who need location tracking, especially useful when you prepare kids for school and can't rely on the child carrying or managing their own phone.
Key strengths:
GPS watch support
SOS button on child's device
Sound-around audio check
Geofenced arrival alerts
Route and visited-places history
Free plan available
Findmykids is built for physical safety, not digital habits. There's no screen time limits, no app blocking, and no content filtering, so it won't help with managing what a child does on a device, only where they are.
It also depends on the child's device staying cooperative. Background location can be disabled on some phones, indoor accuracy drops noticeably, and the app can drain 5 to 15 percent of battery a day, so a determined teenager has more room to work around it than a younger child with a GPS watch does.
Free plans include the SOS button and two geofence zones; premium runs $2.99/mo, $16.99/yr, or $25.99 as a one-time lifetime purchase, with a 3-day trial on paid plans.
Choose Findmykids when: you're tracking a child too young to manage their own phone and need a GPS watch option, not just an app.
What about messaging-app location sharing?
Messaging apps all treat location as a temporary attachment, not a standing feature. The difference vary more than you may initially realize.
WhatsApp gives you up to 8 hours of live sharing inside a chat, Messenger caps out at just 60 minutes, and Telegram is the outlier: you can share "until you turn it off."
Telegram is also the only one of the group with proximity alerts, pinging everyone in a chat when two shared locations move within a set radius of each other.
Signal, ironically, doesn't have live location sharing at all. For an app built entirely around privacy, it's telling that location tracking isn't something they've prioritized, and it says something about how niche this use case is once you step outside dedicated safety apps.
None of these options do geofencing, arrival alerts, location history, or an SOS button, because the location is a message, not a monitored feed. Once the share expires, there's no record and no way to know when someone actually got where they were going. WhatsApp and Messenger are also both owned by Meta, and while the location content itself is end-to-end encrypted, the metadata, who shared with whom, when, how often, still gets logged the way any other Meta activity does.
For a single check-in, any of these will work fine and cost nothing. But treating a messaging app's live location as your family's actual safety net is a mistake: it only works if everyone remembers to open the share manually, every single time, with nothing watching in between.
iPhone↔Android Location Sharing Apps: Does Location Sharing Actually Work Across Platforms?
Not every location sharing app is the same for a mixed-device household.
Find My is the worst offender for mixed households: an Android user can view a shared iPhone's location, but only through a browser link, with no app, no push notifications, and none of the background-tracking parity an iPhone user gets.
Google Maps is the one genuinely reliable free option, since it works identically on both platforms
The dedicated apps in this list, HeyPolo, Life360, and iSharing, are the ones actually built cross-platform from day one rather than retrofitted onto it, so features like groups, alerts, and precision controls behave the same regardless of which phone someone's on.
Which location-sharing app is right for you?
Ultimately, choosing the best location-sharing app comes down to what matters most to you.
If you want an app that balances privacy, flexibility, and functionality, HeyPolo gives you more control over what you share, when you share it, and who gets to see it.
For expansive monitoring features and driving insights, Life360 is worth a closer look. Meanwhile, Google Maps and Apple Find My both work well for casual, free location sharing. iSharing keeps things simple too, though you may need to pay for access to additional features.

FAQ
What's the best location-tracking app?
The best location sharing app, where everyone controls what they share, when they share it, and who can see them - HeyPolo is the strongest option.
Unlike most apps that default to always-on tracking, HeyPolo lets each person decide whether to share their exact location or just a general area, set time windows for sharing, and manage visibility separately across different groups. One subscription covers your whole family, with no per-person fees. And unlike Life360, your data is never sold to third parties.
For families who want to stay connected without it feeling like surveillance, HeyPolo is the most complete option at this price point.
Is there an app better than Life360?
Whether there’s an app better than Life360 depends on your priorities. If you’re looking for Life360 alternatives that let you stay connected with family and friends without constant tracking, HeyPolo is a strong contender focused on consent-based sharing. If you want something simple and free for occasional location sharing, Google Maps or Apple Find My are good options.
Is Google Maps or Life360 better?
It really depends on what you’re looking for. Google Maps is better if you want straightforward, free location sharing on a platform that feels familiar. Life360 is better if you want wide-ranging family-focused features and driving insights with more structured tracking tools.
