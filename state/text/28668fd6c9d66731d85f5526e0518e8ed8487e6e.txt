What is a driving score and what does it mean?
A driving score is a rating of how you drove on a trip. In HeyPolo it runs from 1–10, and a higher number means better driving. Speeding, braking, acceleration, cornering, and phone use all feed into it. Each trip is compared with your own past scores, and other people in your group do not factor in.
A driver score works as a running record of your habits behind the wheel. One trip gives you a number, and a week of trips gives you a pattern you can act on.
HeyPolo built the score to help each driver improve, using the collective experience of the people in their group. Shaming has no part in it.
Which driving behaviors count toward the score?
HeyPolo works as a driving behavior app that looks at five things on every trip: speeding, braking, acceleration, cornering, and phone use. Each one contributes to that trip's score, and the trip scores roll up into your weekly average. Together they give a fuller picture of a drive than speed alone.

Where does a good driving score fall in the 1–10 range?
110
The driving score range runs from 1 to 10, and higher is better. HeyPolo measures you against your own past scores and not against other people in your group, so a good score is one that holds steady or climbs for you. Someone else's eight does not change what your six means.
Your own trend tells you more than any single number. Several weeks of scores near the top of the scale suggest your habits are working, and a dip points you to that week's report. Because the comparison is with your own history, every driver has room to improve at any score.
Why does my score change so much on the first few drives?
Scores can be inconsistent for the first several drives. HeyPolo is still learning how you drive, and it builds its picture from your own past scores. The numbers settle as more trips come in, so it helps to read the early ones as a starting point.
A new driver's first week may look uneven for the same reason.
How does the weekly driver report work?
Each trip gets its own score, and HeyPolo averages those scores over the week into a weekly driver report. The report shows the week's highlights, any incidents, and the other factors that influenced that week's score, so you get the story behind the number.
HeyPolo turns a week of separate trips into one summary, which makes it a driving report app you can read at a glance. It also works as a driving analysis app, since the report points to the highlights and incidents behind your average. You can go through it on your own, and talk it over with the people you have chosen to share your score with.
Who can see my driving score?
Anyone in a group can see a driver's score, but only when that driver decides to share it with that group. Sharing is your call as the driver, and the people you share with see the score you chose to show them.
Sharing is where the group's experience comes in. A parent or a friend who has been driving for years can look at a week with you and point to what they have learned. Your score stays measured against your own past, so there is no leaderboard in your group.
Is HeyPolo a safe driving app for new drivers?
HeyPolo gives a new driver a score for every trip and a weekly report on what shaped it. The family can help with everything they have learned on the road, when the driver chooses to share. That makes HeyPolo a safe driving app built around improving over time, though it cannot promise anyone a safe drive.
If you are choosing a safe driver app for someone who has just started driving, the early scores will be uneven while HeyPolo learns how they drive. The conversation worth having is about the trend over a few weeks, and the driver stays in charge of who sees the score, so it is a conversation they have agreed to.
Will riding as a passenger change my driving score?
By default, HeyPolo does not distinguish a driver from a passenger, so a ride in someone else's car (an Uber, say) can register driving events. You can mark yourself as a passenger for that trip. The risky driving event still shows up, and it does not count against your score, which keeps the score a record of your own driving.

Frequently asked questions
Does a safe driving score mean I'm a safe driver?
A safe driving score in HeyPolo is a rating of five habits: speeding, braking, acceleration, cornering, and phone use. A higher score tells you those habits are improving. It says nothing about the road ahead, and HeyPolo does not promise anyone a safe drive.
What's the best safe driving app for a family?
That depends on what you want from it. HeyPolo scores each trip from 1–10 on speeding, braking, acceleration, cornering, and phone use, measures you against your own past scores, and leaves it to the driver to decide who sees the result. A weekly report explains what shaped the week, and the plan starts at $3.99 a month billed annually. If you want feedback a driver has agreed to, HeyPolo is built for that.
Can someone see my driving score without me knowing?
No. Anyone in a group can see your score only after you decide to share it with that group.
Does HeyPolo detect crashes as well?
Yes. If your phone senses a crash, you have 60 seconds to dismiss a false alarm before the alert goes out. After that, the alert reaches everyone in every group you belong to, plus your emergency contacts. You can still dismiss it later, but by then it has already been sent.
How long does HeyPolo keep my driving data?
HeyPolo keeps driving scores and driving history for 30 days. The privacy policy says geolocation and trip data are processed for 30 days or until consent is withdrawn, whichever comes first.
Is my driving data sold?
No. Your driving and location data is never sold or monetized.
