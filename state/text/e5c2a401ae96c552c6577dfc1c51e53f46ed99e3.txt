How does a speed alert app work?
HeyPolo sends a speed alert the first time on a drive that the driver goes over the speed limit. The driver has to choose to share their speed data first, and each person in the group chooses whether they want to be alerted. Tapping the alert opens that driver's driving report. When the drive ends, the driver gets a summary of their own.

What counts as speeding in a speed limit alert app?
In HeyPolo, speeding means driving at a speed that local police can warn or fine you for. The limit for each road comes from local maps, and the alert works on any speed limit, so nobody has to type in a number.
A speed limit app is only useful if its idea of speeding matches the law where the driver actually is. HeyPolo uses the limit for the road under the driver, wherever in the world that road is, and sends the alert the first time the driver goes over it on a given drive.
Who decides what gets shared and who gets alerted?
The driver decides. Nobody in the group gets speed alerts for a drive until the driver chooses to share their speed data, and the driver picks when. Once they do, each person in the group decides for themselves whether to be alerted when that driver starts to speed. The driver gets a summary of the drive after they have stopped.
Each person in the group makes a separate choice. A parent may want the alert, a sibling may not, and the driver still decides whether anyone sees speed data at all.

Can speed alerts help a family talk about teen speeding?
They can. A teenager who chooses to share speed data gives their parents the option to be alerted the first time a drive goes over the limit. The teen gets their own summary after the drive, so each of them has something concrete to talk about.
Sharing starts with the teenager's yes, so the first conversation happens before any alert does. After that, an alert gives you one specific drive to bring up, and your teen has a summary of that drive too.

When might a speed alert not arrive?
Speed alerts need a GPS connection and a cell tower connection. With both, alerts go out. In a long tunnel, or when a driver is stuck inside one in a traffic jam, the connection can drop for a while, so a temporary outage is expected there.

FAQ
Does the driver have to share speed data?
No. The driver decides if and when to share their speed data. Until they do, nobody in the group gets speed alerts for their drives.
Can I choose not to get speed alerts?
Yes. Anyone in the group can decide for themselves whether to be alerted when a driver starts to speed.
What does the driver see after a drive?
A summary of the drive, once they have stopped driving.
What happens when I tap a speed alert?
The notification opens that driver's driving report in the app.

Driving history is kept for 30 days. Per the privacy policy, geolocation and trip data are processed for 30 days or until consent is withdrawn, whichever comes first.
Is my speed data sold?
No. Your location and driving data is never sold or monetized, and it is not handed to advertisers or data brokers.
