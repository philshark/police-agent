Does Airplane Mode Turn Off Location Tracking
At 11:15 pm a few months ago, I opened a family map and saw a dot parked at an intersection nine minutes from my sister's apartment. It hadn't moved in 40 minutes. No text, no answered calls, just a timestamp getting older while I stared at it. She'd switched her phone to airplane mode on the train and fallen asleep, which is the boring answer to most frozen pins. The question I typed into my phone that night was "does airplane mode turn off location tracking?" The answer surprised me. The switch stops your phone sending anything out, and the part that works out where you are keeps running underneath. Most people hit that switch because they want an hour of quiet. Meanwhile the phone keeps listening to satellites and writing down coordinates, and the people who care about you are looking at a map that stopped. Those are two different problems, and they need different fixes. Does airplane mode turn off location tracking? No. Airplane mode cuts everything your phone sends out wirelessly, which means calls, texts, mobile data and, by default, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. The part that works out your position keeps going. Your phone can put itself on the map with zero bars, because satellite positioning never needed your carrier. It's accurate, too. Under the US government's standard for civil satellite positioning, good receivers have been achieving horizontal accuracy of 3 meters or better 95% of the time. Phones do worse indoors or between tall buildings, so treat three meters as the best case, and don't expect it in a parking garage. Even so, on a clear day your phone knows which side of the street you're on, airplane mode or not. [Infographic in Section 2: Diagram showing two-way cellular and Wi-Fi transmissions being blocked by an airplane mode barrier, while one-way GPS satellite signals pass straight down to the phone's receiver unimpeded] Radios talk back, receivers only listen The hardware explains it. Your phone's cellular chip, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth all send as well as receive. They hold conversations with towers, routers and headphones, and those outgoing signals are what airplane mode switches off, so they can't interfere with the plane's radio equipment. GPS works the other way round. The receiver in your phone is like the FM radio in a car. It picks up signals that satellites broadcast whether anyone is listening or not, and it never sends anything back. There's nothing for airplane mode to switch off, so it leaves the receiver alone. What airplane mode does stop is the record your carrier keeps. The US Supreme Court showed how big that record gets in 2018, when it noted that wireless carriers had handed over 12,898 cell-site location points covering 127 days of one man's movements, roughly 101 points a day. That's a location point about every 15 minutes, all day, for four months. Each one was logged because his phone connected to a tower, which it does just by being switched on. Turn on airplane mode and those connections stop for as long as it stays on. The one place you might notice a difference is speed. Without mobile data, your phone can't download the satellite data that usually speeds up its first fix, so it can take longer to lock on. Once it does, the position is as good as ever. Turn-by-turn directions stop, rideshare apps can't find you, and emergency calls lose your automatic location. Most people don't want to give that up all day. There's a middle option. FAQ
Can someone track my phone if it's on airplane mode?
Not live. A family member or app can't see you moving, because the phone can't send data to the network. Apple's Find My network is the exception. If Bluetooth is switched back on while airplane mode is running, nearby Apple devices can pass an approximate location to the owner's account.
What does a location-sharing app show when your phone is in airplane mode?
Your last known location, with the time your phone last had a mobile or Wi-Fi connection. Your group sees you as offline or paused, and nothing updates until you reconnect.
Can 911 find me if my phone is on airplane mode?
Calling 911 overrides airplane mode on current iPhone and Android phones and reconnects to the nearest tower. How accurate your location is depends on the phone, the terrain and the local dispatch center, so it isn't guaranteed to be precise. [CHECK BEFORE PUBLISHING]
Does airplane mode turn off GPS navigation on Google Maps or Apple Maps?
No. The GPS receiver keeps working, so the blue dot still follows you. What you lose is new map data. Without a connection, the app can't download new areas, so download your maps before you go offline.
Does turning off location services save battery compared to airplane mode?
Airplane mode saves more. The mobile radio uses a lot of power keeping in touch with distant towers, so switching it off makes the bigger difference. The GPS receiver only uses a modest amount while it's working out your position.
