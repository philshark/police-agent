Does Airplane Mode Turn Off Location Tracking
Simply put, no. Airplane mode does not turn off Location Services
Saras
8 min read•October 19, 2026
No. Airplane mode cuts everything your phone sends out wirelessly, which means calls, texts, mobile data and, by default, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. The part that works out your position keeps going. Your phone can put itself on the map with zero bars, because satellite positioning never needed your carrier.
It's accurate, too. Under the US government's standard for civil satellite positioning, good receivers have been or better 95% of the time. Phones do worse indoors or between tall buildings, so treat three meters as the best case, and don't expect it in a parking garage. Even so, on a clear day your phone knows which side of the street you're on, airplane mode or not.
Radios talk back, receivers only listen
Your phone's cellular chip, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth all send as well as receive. They hold conversations with towers, routers and headphones, and those outgoing signals are what airplane mode switches off, so they can't interfere with the plane's radio equipment.
GPS works the other way round. The receiver in your phone is like the FM radio in a car. It picks up signals that satellites broadcast whether anyone is listening or not, and it never sends anything back. There's nothing for airplane mode to switch off, so it leaves the receiver alone.
What airplane mode does stop is the record your carrier keeps.
The US Supreme Court showed how big that record gets in 2018, when it noted that wireless carriers had  covering 127 days of one man's movements, roughly 101 points a day. That's a location point about every 15 minutes, all day, for four months. Each one was logged because his phone connected to a tower, which it does just by being switched on.
Turn on airplane mode and those connections stop for as long as it stays on.
That’s also why airplane mode saves so much battery. The mobile radio is one of the biggest drains on a phone, and it explains most of  .
The one place you might notice a difference is speed. Without mobile data, your phone can't download the satellite data that usually speeds up its first fix, so it can take longer to lock on. Once it does, the position is as good as ever.
Your phone recording a route and someone seeing it are separate things
Your phone knowing where you are and your phone telling someone where you are happen in two different places.
Airplane mode cleanly stops the second one.
A location-sharing app has no way to reach the internet, so it can't send an update. Your group sees your pin at the last place your phone had data, with a timestamp showing when the connection dropped.
The recording keeps going.
Walk, drive or run offline with downloaded maps and your navigation app will draw your route on the screen in real time. Fitness apps and the phone's own timeline features save those coordinates to the phone.
Take a three-hour train ride in airplane mode and your phone can still log the whole route. Turn airplane mode off, or join the station Wi-Fi, and those records can sync to servers you've never thought about.
Phones are chatty when they're connected.
A 2021 study by Douglas J. Leith at Trinity College Dublin found that idle phones, barely set up and left untouched,  every 4.5 minutes on average, and each connection revealed the phone's IP address. That's about 13 check-ins an hour from a phone sitting on a desk. Airplane mode pauses all of it, and reconnecting sends the backlog.
Bluetooth, offline finding, and the beacons that outlast a shutdown
On current iPhones and Android phones, airplane mode turns Wi-Fi and Bluetooth off by default, then remembers if you turn either one back on. Plenty of people do, usually for headphones or a watch.
The airplane icon stays in the status bar while Bluetooth quietly reconnects, and that reopens one of the ways your location can leave the phone.
Apple's Find My network uses exactly that route. According to the Apple Platform Security Guide, offline finding is on by default for devices running iOS 13 or later with Find My iPhone enabled, and a disconnected iPhone keeps sending out low-energy Bluetooth signals.
Any Apple device that passes by picks up the encrypted signal and sends an approximate location, with a timestamp, back to the owner's account. Your phone doesn't need a connection for any of it, which is how it’s possible to at all.

How to actually stop location tracking on iPhone and Android
If you want the phone to stop working out where you are, the setting you need is Location Services. The network switches won't do it.
On iPhone,
Open Settings, tap Privacy and Security, tap Location Services, and switch it off.
On Android
Open Settings, tap Location, and turn off the main switch. Menu names change a little between versions and brands, so look for the word Location.
Turn-by-turn directions stop, rideshare apps can’t find you, and emergency calls lose your automatic location. So does any that sends your position to family when you need help. Most people don’t want to give that up all day.
There's a middle option. Go through your apps one by one and set them to While Using the App, so your maps still work and other apps stop checking where you are when you're not using them. Few people ever open that screen, and if you do, you may find a weather app or a shopping app set to Always.

FAQ
Can someone track my phone if it's on airplane mode?
Not live. A family member or app can't see you moving, because the phone can't send data to the network. Apple's Find My network is the exception. If Bluetooth is switched back on while airplane mode is running, nearby Apple devices can pass an approximate location to the owner's account.
What does a location-sharing app show when your phone is in airplane mode?
Your last known location, with the time your phone last had a mobile or Wi-Fi connection. Your group sees you as offline or paused, and nothing updates until you reconnect.
Can 911 find me if my phone is on airplane mode?
Calling 911 overrides airplane mode on current iPhone and Android phones and reconnects to the nearest tower. How accurate your location is depends on the phone, the terrain and the local dispatch center, so it isn't guaranteed to be precise. [CHECK BEFORE PUBLISHING]
Does airplane mode turn off GPS navigation on Google Maps or Apple Maps?
No. The GPS receiver keeps working, so the blue dot still follows you. What you lose is new map data. Without a connection, the app can't download new areas, so download your maps before you go offline.
Does turning off location services save battery compared to airplane mode?
Airplane mode saves more. The mobile radio uses a lot of power keeping in touch with distant towers, so switching it off makes the bigger difference. The GPS receiver only uses a modest amount while it's working out your position.
