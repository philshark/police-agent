The first year of driving, without the interrogation
A new driver is still building experience, both behind the wheel and in showing you it's going fine. Most parents don't need a play-by-play of every drive. Knowing the car got there safely covers it, most nights.
Arrival alerts handle that automatically. A notification when the car reaches school, practice, or the driveway means nobody has to send or wait for a text about how the drive went. For the teen, that's one less check-in to remember. For the parent, it's the same reassurance without having to ask for it.
Speed alerts work the same way. They're built for a quick conversation once a week, looking at the same numbers together, not a report card on every single drive. HeyPolo shows the teen exactly what the parent sees, so nothing about their driving is being decided behind their back.

How to use this in a teen's first driving year
Three habits that work better than checking the map at every red light:
1
Set the places, not the pings
Add school, practice and home so arrivals come to you automatically. That's the reassurance covered, without asking about the drive.
2
Review speed together, weekly
Look at it once with them rather than reacting live. Wet roads, slip roads, night driving, those are useful conversations, and a running commentary isn't.
3
Agree it out loud
Decide together what's shared and for how long. Sharing can run for the drive and stop afterwards, and a teen who agreed to it is a teen who keeps it on.

Frequently asked questions
Does HeyPolo have crash detection?
We won't claim it as a live feature here. What is available today is arrival alerts, speed monitoring, and a one-tap SOS that sends an exact location to your group, and that's what we'd suggest planning around.
How does HeyPolo compare with Life360 for driving?
Honestly, Life360's paid tiers have a more built-out driving package, including roadside assistance and emergency dispatch, which HeyPolo doesn't offer. What HeyPolo offers instead is speed awareness without selling anyone's location data, precision control, and one flat family price rather than tiered upsells.
Won't speed monitoring feel like being spied on?
It can, if it's used as a running commentary. It works better as a weekly look at the same screen together, framed around building experience: motorway lanes, wet roads, night driving. Your teen sees exactly what you see, which is what keeps it from feeling like a report card.
Can my teen turn sharing off?
Yes. Private mode pauses their sharing while keeping them in the group, and everyone can see it's paused. Nothing happens quietly, and that visibility is largely why teens keep the app past month one.
Are new drivers really at higher risk?
New drivers are still building experience, and less experience does mean more risk than a seasoned driver carries. We're not going to attach a number to that here without a properly cited current source.
Do arrival alerts need any effort from my teen?
No. Once the places are set, the notification goes out when they get there. No reply, no unlocking a phone while parking.
Is my teen's driving data sold to insurers or advertisers?
No. Location and driving data isn't sold, monetized, or shared with advertisers or data brokers, and it's encrypted in transit and at rest.
Is there an extra charge for driving features?
No. There are no tiers: speed monitoring, arrival alerts and SOS are all in the single plan, and everyone you invite joins free.
