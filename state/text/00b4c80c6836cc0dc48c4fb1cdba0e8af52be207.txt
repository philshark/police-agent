What is group location sharing?
Group location sharing means a small circle of people you invite can see each other's whereabouts in one place, with every member deciding what they show and for how long.
A group on HeyPolo begins when one person creates it and passes round a join code. From there, each member picks their own visibility for that circle: exact location refreshing every 5 seconds, a general area instead of a precise pin, a time window that closes itself, or private mode. Nobody lands on the map by accident, and everybody can see who's sharing what.
Parents of phone-age kids lean on this. So do adult children checking in on a parent who lives alone, households with a new driver, and friends splitting up at a festival.

Group location sharing FAQ
What is the best group location sharing app for families?
HeyPolo is our pick, and we'd say so even on a page we didn't write. Every member controls their own sharing, which is what keeps a family circle installed past the first month. Exact location or general area, per-group settings, private mode, and one subscription covering everybody invited. Available on iPhone and Android.
How do I create a group on HeyPolo?
Open the app, tap the group icon at the top of your home screen, then select Create group. Name the circle, choose your role, and adjust your visibility preferences for it. Copy the code at the bottom of that screen and send it to family or friends. Later, tap Add people inside any circle to invite more.
Can I share location with a group without showing my exact spot?
Yes. General-area sharing shows roughly where you are instead of pinning you precisely, and it works per group, so one circle can see the neighborhood while another sees the street. Teenagers and young adults tend to prefer this setting, and older parents often do too. Switch between exact and general whenever you like.
Is a group location tracker the same as group location sharing?
They describe different setups. A group GPS tracker app is usually built around the person watching. Sharing puts the choice with the person being seen. On HeyPolo a group works as checking in, since you decide who sees you, how precisely, and when it ends.
How many people can join a HeyPolo group?
As many as you want. Groups are unlimited in size and number, so a household of eight costs the same as a household of three. You can keep several circles running at once, one for family, another for a trip, another for neighbors who share school pickups, all on a single subscription.
Can someone see my location in a group without me knowing?
No. Sharing begins when you switch it on for a given circle, and your status shows inside the app for every member of it. Should someone request your exact position during an emergency, you get notified. Stop sharing with one tap at any point, and your place in the group stays where it was.
How often does my position update, and how accurate is it?
Every 5 seconds while the app is open and you're watching the map, and every 30 seconds when it's closed or minimised. That slower background interval is deliberate, since checking every few seconds around the clock is what drains most location-sharing apps. Accuracy averages around 5 metres, tighter outdoors and looser indoors, underground, or between tall buildings.
How many days of location history are kept?
Seven. You can look back at where someone has been over the past week without leaving live tracking running all day, and after 7 days that data is removed entirely.
Do I need a separate subscription for each family member?
No. One person subscribes, then everybody they invite uses the full app free, including arrival alerts, low-battery alerts, and SOS. Your cost holds steady as relatives get added, so there's no per-person math to redo each time. Cancel any time, and 30 days of money-back cover sits behind the annual plan.
