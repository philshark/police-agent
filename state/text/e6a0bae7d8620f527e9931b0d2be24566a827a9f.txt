Pedestrian Safety Month 2026: Fewer U.S. Deaths But More Accidents
October is Pedestrian Safety Month. While fewer people on foot are being killed in the US, a growing share of incidents end in death. Most happen after sunset on busy roads. Here is what the numbers say and how to keep track of loved ones on their walks.

It is just gone six on a Wednesday. Your mom is on her third lap of the block with the dog, in her good coat because the evenings are getting a bit more brisk these last few days.
You know because she told you at noon, in a voice message that ran two minutes long.
You are four hundred miles away, stirring soup in a kitchen she has only seen on video calls.
You’re waiting for a phone call that has not arrived.
She has walked that block for years, and you still want to know when she is back inside.
October is Pedestrian Safety Month, and the most useful thing most of us can do with it is small: cross at designated intersections, never assume a driver has seen you, and ask the people we worry about which stretch of their walk feels least safe.
Among people 65 and older killed in traffic crashes in 2024, 19% were pedestrians, so a dog walk like your mom's is part of that number.

In 2024, 7,080 pedestrians were killed in US traffic crashes. That equals about 19 deaths a day, or one every 74 minutes. An estimated 71,635 pedestrians suffered injuries, or one every 7 minutes, according to the report from NHTSA. The total count fell, but something underneath it climbed.

Fewer pedestrians are dying, and the crashes are deadlier
The good news: The total number of fatalities has dropped.
Federal figures show 7,080 pedestrian deaths in 2024, down 3.9% in 2023. The Governors Highway Safety Association projects a 7% drop from 2024 and the third straight yearly decline.
That projection remains 5% above the 6,412 recorded in 2019, according to the report from GHSA.
Non-fatal injuries moved the other way though, with an estimated 71,635 pedestrians injured in 2024, up 5.0% from 68,241 in 2023.
The critical change is the severity of each collision. The share of pedestrian crashes that ended in a death doubled between 2015 and 2024.
Pedestrians accounted for 18% of all traffic deaths in 2024 while making up only 3% of traffic injuries.
The AAA Foundation reports pedestrian deaths rose 83% from their 2009 low to 7,522 in 2022, the highest count since 1981. Pedestrians grew from 11% of crash deaths during the 2000 to 2009 period to 18% in 2022.
The reports document this pattern clearly without establishing a single underlying cause. As Jonathan Adkins, Chief Executive Officer of GHSA, noted in a , "the only acceptable number of traffic deaths is zero."
Most deaths happen after dark on busy main roads
The risk concentrates in specific physical environments and lighting conditions.
In 2024, 76% of pedestrian deaths occurred in darkness, 20% in daylight, 2% at dusk, and 2% at dawn.
Furthermore, 73% of fatal pedestrian crashes happened away from intersections, while 18% occurred at intersections.
The AAA Foundation found that virtually the entire increase in pedestrian deaths since 2009 occurred in darkness, primarily on urban arterial roads. Arterials are major multi-lane roads designed to carry vehicle traffic between neighborhoods.
In three case-study cities analyzed by the AAA Foundation between roughly 2013 and 2023, arterials accounted for 81% of fatal pedestrian crashes in Charlotte and 85% in Memphis.
Arterials accounted for 95% of fatal pedestrian crashes in Albuquerque, excluding interstates.
Principal arterials accounted for 37.9% of 2024 pedestrian deaths with a known road type, and minor arterials represented 25.1%, making up about 63% combined. In 62.2% of fatal pedestrian crashes in 2024, no sidewalk was noted, compared to 64.8% in 2023.
Individual cities show how severe this concentration can become. Fatal pedestrian crashes in Memphis nearly quadrupled between 2014 and 2022, while non-fatal injury crashes involving pedestrians rose 11%.
Urban areas accounted for 84% of all pedestrian crashes in 2024, but rural crashes proved far more lethal. According to GHSA, 15.1% of rural pedestrian crashes were fatal, compared to 9.2% of urban crashes.

Collision types between 2019 and 2024 show stark differences in outcomes. Data indicates that crashes where a pedestrian crossed an expressway were fatal 56.7% of the time.
Collisions with a vehicle traveling straight on standard roads were fatal 11.9% of the time, compared to 1.1% when the vehicle was turning.
The deadliest hours are the ones when families are walking home
The clock matters as much as the street layout. The deadliest three-hour window in 2024 was 6:00 pm to 8:59 pm, accounting for 24% of pedestrian deaths, followed by 9:00 pm to 11:59 pm at 23%.
During fall and winter months, the 6:00 pm to 8:59 pm window rises to 29% of all pedestrian fatalities.

Data for younger children provides an encouraging contrast to adult trends.
In 2024, 172 children aged 14 and younger were killed as pedestrians, representing 17% of child traffic fatalities and 2% of total pedestrian deaths, down 27% from 2015.
Child pedestrian incidents peak earlier in the day. Among child pedestrian deaths in 2024, 28% occurred between 3:00 pm and 5:59 pm and 22% between 6:00 pm and 8:59 pm, with 51% occurring during daylight. Structuring an organized helps manage those daylight hours before evening dusk sets in.
School transportation safety records reflect a similar dynamic around boarding zones.
Between 2015 and 2024, 162 pedestrians died in school-transportation-related crashes, exceeding the 119 occupants killed riding inside school vehicles.

Speed and vehicle height change what a crash costs
Vehicle velocity and front-end geometry determine the physical force delivered during an impact.
The AAA Foundation established an impact speed risk curve based on crash data. The average risk of death for a struck pedestrian reaches 10% at 23 mph and 25% at 32 mph.
Fatality risk climbs to 50% at 42 mph, 75% at 50 mph, and 90% at 58 mph. Those calculations drew from 1994 to 1998 crash investigations.
Vehicle dimensions amplify those speed effects. In a 2024 test series by the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety, at 27 mph a median-height passenger car produced a 60% probability of moderate injury. That same car produced a 30% probability of serious injury.
A median-height pickup truck with a blunt front profile produced a 62% probability of serious injury at the exact same speed, according to the .
Vehicle fleet composition reflects this shift toward larger profiles. Among 2024 pedestrian fatalities where the vehicle type was known, light trucks including SUVs, pickups, and vans accounted for 50.4% of striking vehicles, while passenger cars accounted for 36.4%.
Police recorded speeding in 8.1% of fatal pedestrian crashes in 2024, climbing to 11.9% on local neighborhood streets and dropping to 4.6% on expressways.

Halloween adds a short seasonal spike
Halloween brings a brief surge in evening pedestrian activity across residential neighborhoods.
A University of British Columbia analysis of 42 years of federal crash data reported a 43% higher risk of pedestrian death on Halloween compared to surrounding evenings.
The study noted that the highest risk falls between 5:00 pm and 8:00 pm.
In 2026, Halloween falls on a Saturday, October 31, the evening before standard time begins. Setting a temporary sharing window for a trick-or-treat outing lets parents confirm when a neighborhood loop finishes without checking in repeatedly.
Parents and grandparents are walking too
Pedestrian vulnerability increases significantly with age. Among adults aged 65 and older killed in traffic crashes in 2024, 19% were pedestrians, totaling 1,535 individuals. Pedestrians also accounted for 23% of all traffic fatalities among individuals aged 40 to 69.
Physical resilience declines over time. The AAA Foundation found that severe injury or fatality risk for a 70-year-old pedestrian struck at 25 mph equals the risk for a 30-year-old struck at 35 mph.
Those findings drew from 1994 to 1998 crash data.

What the reports say about phones and alcohol
Distraction and alcohol appear regularly in collision records, though neither factors as a universal explanation.
Reported distraction increased for both motorists and pedestrians between 2019 and 2024, with the proportion of distracted pedestrians surpassing distracted drivers in official records.
Distraction remains substantially underreported and that recent increases partly reflect stricter state reporting protocols.
Alcohol involvement by the driver, the pedestrian, or both, defined as a blood alcohol concentration of .01 or higher, was recorded in 43% of fatal pedestrian crashes in 2024.
In 27% of those crashes, the pedestrian had a BAC of .08 or higher, while the driver had a BAC of .08 or higher in 15%, according to NHTSA.
Walking home after a social gathering removes the danger of driving impaired, but navigating dark streets on foot still demands situational awareness. Having a active during an evening walk gives friends or family a clear view of the journey home.
Can the US learn from other countries?
International comparisons demonstrate that pedestrian deaths are not inevitable.
By 2022, most comparable developed countries had reduced pedestrian fatality rates by 70% to more than 90% from 1982 levels, while the United States achieved a reduction of roughly 30%.
The current US pedestrian death rate is two to five times higher than many peer nations and nearly ten times higher than rates in Norway and Sweden.
Infrastructure improvements and targeted traffic calming show immediate results in American communities:
Salt Lake City recorded a decline in pedestrian deaths from 22 to 14 after lowering municipal speed limits in 2023. A $3.4 million roadway redesign along a two-mile corridor in Ohio replaced seven traffic signals with seven mini-roundabouts. Speeding vehicles dropped from 63% to 17%, and injury crashes fell by 69%.
West Virginia implemented the Target Red pilot program in Beckley, reducing red-light violations and associated crashes by 60%. On a 21-mile section of the Pacific Coast Highway in Malibu, average speeds dropped 7% and harsh-braking incidents fell 14%.
Vehicle safety standards are also evolving. Federal regulations will require pedestrian automatic emergency braking systems on all new passenger vehicles by 2029.

Frequently asked questions
When is Pedestrian Safety Month?
NHTSA established Pedestrian Safety Month as a national observance in October 2020. It runs throughout the entire month of October across the United States.
What time of day are most pedestrians killed?
The three-hour period from 6:00 pm to 8:59 pm accounts for 24% of all pedestrian fatalities, followed by 9:00 pm to 11:59 pm at 23%. In 2024, 76% of all fatal pedestrian crashes occurred in darkness.
Are pedestrian deaths going up or down?
Total pedestrian fatalities have declined for three consecutive years through 2025 projections, though counts remain above 2019 levels. However, the proportion of pedestrian crashes resulting in a death climbed from 4.0% in 2015 to 10.1% in 2024.
